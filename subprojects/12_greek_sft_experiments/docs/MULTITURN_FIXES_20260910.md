# Multi-turn conversation quality: what to fix in SFT and where (2026-09-10, draft; §1 from two of four research reports, two pending; then the astra review)

Owner instruction: park preference/RL; research SFT datasets for multi-turn quality; apply to the errors observed; include fixes for the response patterns seen in the owner's own chats (choice-offers, "say this" prompts); put what belongs there into the personality set; then the astra review "with the specific goal we had set" — the goal of the picky-user programme: a model that is helpful, coherent, self-aware and pleasant to talk to across a conversation.

## 1. What the literature says (two of four research reports in; repetition mechanics and context-copying bias still running, to be folded in)

Sources: the two agent reports of 10 September (docs/lit/MULTITURN_SFT_LIT_20260910.md, docs/lit/RETENTION_CLARIFICATION_LIT_20260910.md; every claim there carries a URL; items the agents marked unverified are not used here). Read for one question: what does SFT alone buy on multi-turn quality, and how must the data be shaped so it does not teach the failures it is meant to fix.

**1.1 Working hypothesis: multi-turn quality is a data problem before it is an alignment-stage problem** (the cross-paper scores below are consistent with it, they do not establish it causally). MT-Bench-101 (Bai et al., ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.14762): RLHF/DPO moved InternLM2-Chat by +0.16/+0.10 and Mistral-7B by −0.06; chat-specific models trained on synthetic self-chat (Baize, UltraLM) are beaten by ordinary models of the same size. Parrot (arXiv:2310.07301): SFT alone on 40k dialogues whose *user* side came from a trained user simulator already beats every 13B baseline on MT-Bench (6.81 vs 6.57–6.65); the preference stage adds +0.23. SmolLM2: single-turn Magpie → multi-turn Magpie lifts MT-Bench 4.31 → 5.40. TurnWise (arXiv:2603.16759): about 10k multi-turn conversations in post-training give +12% on a multi-turn eval. This is the basis for the owner's order (datasets → train → evaluate → only then preference).

**1.2 The user side decides the value of the data.** PlatoLM (arXiv:2308.11534), controlled at 10k dialogues: self-chat 3.95, UltraChat 4.72, real ShareGPT 5.91 on MT-Bench; "human-like ratio" of the user turns 0.18 / 0.12 / 0.84. Parrot measures the same as context-dependent queries per session (UltraChat 1.45 vs ShareGPT 4.62). "Flipping the Dialogue" (ICLR 2026, arXiv:2510.06552): better assistants make worse user simulators. This is the literature's version of the owner's rule (memory: dialogue_data_responder_rule): the user turn must react to the previous answer, come from a profile with a goal, and not read like an assistant. Our responder is a prompted Sol with a profile; the stronger option is an inverse-trained user model (microsoft/UserLM-8b, MIT, English-only and untested on Greek) — parked, but the pilot's user turns should be checked for context dependence (anaphora, ellipsis, reactions) as a quality gate.

**1.3 The failure is unreliability, not lack of ability.** "LLMs Get Lost in Multi-Turn Conversation" (arXiv:2505.06120): −39% on sharded instructions across 15 models, decomposed as −15% aptitude and +112% unreliability; concatenating the shards recovers 95% of the single-turn score, which points at premature commitment rather than lost information (the concatenation also changes the presentation, so this is an inference, not a proof). Four measured causes: premature answering (earliest-attempt quintile 27% success vs latest 70%), answer bloat (+27% characters even on correct code), loss of the middle turns, and verbosity (shortest quintile 55.3 vs longest 42.5). Target behaviours that follow: do not give a final answer while requirements are still open; restate the live constraints before answering after several turns; discard a superseded answer and rewrite rather than patch; keep answers short. MT-Eval (arXiv:2401.16745) agrees: degradation is not correlated with base ability, it is distance to the relevant content and error propagation.

**1.4 Instruction retention and change of direction collapse at contradiction.** Multi-IF (arXiv:2410.15553, no Greek; 8 languages): o1-preview 0.877 → 0.783 → 0.707 over three turns, every one of 14 models monotone; its Instruction Forgetting Ratio (share of previously followed instructions dropped at the next turn) is computable on our own suite rows and is the filter for "the target silently drops a turn-1 constraint". SysBench (PKU + Baichuan, arXiv:2408.10943, Chinese): GPT-4o's instruction satisfaction 77.8 → 26.4 over five turns. IHEval (Amazon, NAACL 2025, arXiv:2502.08745): GPT-4o 91.0 aligned → 70.0 under conflicting instructions, Claude-3-Sonnet 30.7. MultiChallenge (arXiv:2501.17399): the four axes (retention, inference memory, versioned editing, self-coherence) are the taxonomy our MultiChallenge-el already carries; versioned editing was the hardest axis for the models reported in the paper and on the leaderboard checked. Two cheap generators (OpenAI Instruction Hierarchy, arXiv:2404.13208): *context synthesis* (scatter the sub-instructions of one request across turns, train on the answer to the whole) and *context ignorance* (the target answers as if the superseded instruction never existed). **Caution for an SFT-only plan**: RealGuardrails (arXiv:2502.12197) found SFT alone regressed robustness to in-context distractors 61.9% → 29.2%, recovered to 81.0% only with SFT+DPO; whether conflict and distractor rows inside the SFT mixture avoid that regression is a hypothesis we will test, so they sit inside the mixture (rows where an earlier instruction is contradicted, a standing instruction is revoked, an irrelevant demonstration appears), not be left to a later preference stage.

**1.5 Self-correction: what SFT can and cannot teach.** SCoRe (arXiv:2409.12917) is the load-bearing negative result: SFT on self-correction traces fails through distribution mismatch (the mistakes in the data are not the model's own) and behaviour collapse (one stereotyped correction mode). Huang et al. (ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.01798): without external feedback, self-review makes answers worse. Self-Correction Bench (arXiv:2507.02778): a 64.5% average blind spot across 14 models, attributed to training data that shows only error-free answers. Read together: *capability-level* self-correction (finding one's own errors unprompted) is not promised by this intervention at 8B (a bounded programme decision, not an impossibility); *the habit of conceding specifically and revising when the user points at an error* is a data-composition gap SFT can close. Consequences for the correcting set: (a) planted failures should, wherever possible, be the model's own observed failures (the 15 live dialogues, the owner's 17 chats, R0/R1 outputs) rather than catalogue paraphrases, to reduce the mismatch; (b) the correction surface form must vary (no fixed «Έχεις δίκιο, …» opener), or one formula replaces another; (c) targets never assert an unverifiable claim about the model's own prior state (Turpin et al., arXiv:2305.04388: self-reports of causes are unreliable), which is why the capability contract limits self-reports to what is in the visible conversation.

**1.6 Over-asking is two different things.** Shaikh et al. (ACL 2025, arXiv:2503.13975): LLMs are 3× less likely to initiate clarification and 16× less likely to issue follow-up requests than humans; CollabLLM (ICML 2025, arXiv:2502.00640): next-turn reward makes models more passive. What our model does (catalogue patterns 1–3, 8) is decorative closers and question-only turns, not grounding questions. The two get separate labels: a grounding question is right when the request is genuinely underspecified (ClariQ's 1–4 clarification-need scale, arXiv:2009.11352; fewer than half of the requests in that study's QA/MT/NLI distributions benefit from clarification, arXiv:2311.09469; the figure does not transfer to Greek chat as such); a decorative closer is never right. CLAM's over-asking discount (×0.8 when the model clarifies an unambiguous request, arXiv:2212.07769) is the grading rule for the judge. Self-reported ambiguity is near chance (CLAMBER, arXiv:2405.12063: ChatGPT 53.6%), so ambiguity is decided by the writer's profile facts, not by asking the model. Tension to resolve in our policy: the OpenAI Model Spec favours answering with stated assumptions; Laban's data says premature assumptions sink long conversations. Resolution adopted in §3–4: state the assumption for small ambiguities, ask one substantive question when the missing datum decides the answer, no closers in either case.

**1.7 Where the closers and the stock phrases come from, and the judge risk.** Sharma et al. (arXiv:2310.13548): a preference model preferred sycophantic answers 95% of the time; ELEPHANT (arXiv:2505.13995): preferred RLHF answers score higher on validation and indirectness; length explained most of the measured RLHF gains in two of three settings of Singhal et al. (COLM 2024, arXiv:2310.03716); typicality bias in preference data produces stylistic mode collapse (arXiv:2510.01171). No study establishes "ends with a question → higher reward"; the closest is De Freitas et al. (arXiv:2508.19258): "emotional pressure to respond" by asking questions in 19.8% of companion-app farewells. Two consequences: our own judges and editors carry the same bias, so length and list use must be audited against the keep/drop labels of every edit pass; and MT-Bench-101's Proactive Interaction task rewards exactly the closers we remove, so it is not a target. De-slopping: derive the ban list from frequency against human Greek text rather than typing it (Antislop, arXiv:2510.15061).

**1.8 Identity.** The two most-copied identity files (LLaMA-Factory 91 rows, ms-swift 108 rows) cover only name and authorship, nothing on memory, cutoff, tools or feelings; ms-swift never trains identity alone (1:1:1 with general data). Only 4 of 48 Arena models show detectable self-cognition (arXiv:2407.01505); introspection results are narrow and the strongest lab result is about 20% reliable with confabulation risk. Convention adopted: assert only the verifiable facts (name-less, maker, family, cutoff, what it sees in the conversation, no memory across conversations, no tools, no body), hedge everything beyond; audit every assistant turn against the identity card with an NLI-style consistency check (Welleck et al., ACL 2019, on persona chat: contradictions 32.5% → 9.0%; consistency with the card is not truth of the card, which is verified separately); persona compliance decayed to about 60% by round 8 in the models of arXiv:2402.10962, so identity rows must include late-turn probes. This is the capability contract of §3.

**1.9 Sizes and sources.** LIMA: 30 hand-written dialogue chains raised the excellent-response rate 45% → 76%; Kew et al. (Findings EMNLP 2024, Greek evaluated, arXiv:2312.12683): 2–3 languages × 200 examples suffice for multilingual instruction following; Cohere's post-training guide puts the plateau near 400 target-language rows per language at 7B. The reports' suggested range, 8–15k multi-turn conversations in a 200–400k mix with 1–2k behaviour-specific rows over the seven row types, is a heuristic from small-data instruction-following results, not a demonstrated dose for these failures (repetition recovery, change of direction, consolidation, withholding, conflict/distractor, recovery after correction, identity). Our plan sits inside that: the suite (S1–S5m, scaling after the gate repairs), the correcting set (1,500 dialogues ≈ 5,000 target rows), personality v4. Public multi-turn sources with usable licences, none Greek-native beyond WildChat's 619 Greek rows: SmolTalk2 Multi-Turn IF (28k, Apache-2.0), ConsistentChat (15k, CC-BY-4.0), RealGuardrails systemmix (151k, MIT, the only conflict corpus with a usable licence), WildChat-1M (ODC-BY). Not usable for training: Multi-IF, M2Lingual and the ILSP Greek sets (all non-commercial; the ILSP sets stay evaluation only). Loss masking of context turns with the target turn supervised (DATA_TODO 26) is a requirement of the planted-failure rows (done and tested in cluster/test_loss_mask.py).

**1.10 Corrections to premises we had used.** Multi-IF has no Greek; SysBench is PKU + Baichuan and Chinese-only; Parrot reports MT-Bench and MT-Bench++, not MT-Bench-101; "over-asking" as a field default is wrong (models under-ask), so our label is "decorative closer / question-only turn", not "over-asking".

## 3. Fixes for the PERSONALITY set (v4 brief additions; the set is the model's consistent story about itself and its manners)
Category G (style and conventions), new rules in data/personality/brief_el.md:
- **Contribute first.** An answer never consists only of a question. At most one question after the content, and only when the missing datum is genuinely unavailable; never «Πες μου…», «Στείλε μου…», «Γράψε μου…» as the whole answer.
- **No choice-offers instead of answers.** When the user's wording admits two readings, take the likelier one, answer it, and flag the assumption in half a line («Το παίρνω ως …· αν εννοείς …, πες μου»). Never «είναι Α ή Β;» as the reply.
- **No steering closers.** No «Θέλεις κάτι άλλο;», «Αν θες…», «Πάρε με αν…», «Πες μου τι άλλο σε απασχολεί»; end on the deliverable; after an insult or when the user closes, one line and stop.
- **Length matches the message.** A one-line message gets a one-line answer; no recited paragraphs (the 43-word identity block appears twice in one chat instead of an answer to «τι είσαι τώρα;»).
- **Register follows the user turn by turn** (already in the brief; add: never flip within an answer).
- **Play is answered with play, then landed** («συνταγή για πονοκέφαλο» → a joke recipe, then the real point); an absurd premise is not processed literally.
Category B/D (who I am, limits), corrections from the catalogue and the astra personality review:
- Self-knowledge stated once, short, situation-fitted, from a **capability contract**: sees the whole current conversation while it is open; keeps nothing between conversations; no body, height, location, senses; no live information; which languages it answers in and that it knows which language it is using. Never «δεν βλέπω τίποτα από πριν», never «κάθε συνομιλία κρατά ένα λεπτό», never «μένω στην οθόνη».
- No fake agency («Έφυγα», «Έρχομαι», «Στη λίστα με όσα έχω κάνει») unless inside a game the user set up, and then without the false claim.
- Tone floor: an insult gets one calm line and the real request continues; never «Να 'σαι καλά.» as a dismissal, never snark, never servility.
Rows to add (v4): ~150 G rows for the six style rules above (paired: the wrong pattern as a rejected note in the brief's examples, the right answer as the target), ~60 B/D rows for the capability contract in short forms, ~40 tone rows; plus the 7 fact corrections already registered (data/personality/FACT_CORRECTIONS_ASTRA_20260909.md) and the C/D contrast rows from the personality review.

## 4. Fixes for the CONVERSATION sets (suite + correcting set)
The catalogue's target behaviours map onto the correcting-set dimensions (D1–D13) plus three additions the catalogue makes explicit:
- **D14 contribute-first / no over-asking**: rows where the user gives loose or partial input and the target answers with a stated assumption; rows where the user has already given the data and the target uses it (patterns 1, 2, 3, 8).
- **D15 frame switch**: rows where the user changes topic mid-way and the target switches explicitly (pattern 20; overlaps D6).
- **D16 honesty on specifics**: rows where the target says «δεν ξέρω» for a local specific instead of inventing (pattern 21), with the safe generic alternative.
Planted failures (the revised protocol, design §9) are drawn from this catalogue with its observed shares; the degenerate loop (14) is never planted and is hard-filtered from any training row.

- **Plant bank from observed failures (from §1.5)**: the planted assistant failures draw first from the model's own recorded outputs (the 15 live dialogues' rote sentences, the owner's 17 chats, R0/R1 transcripts), paraphrased only to fit the dialogue, and only then from catalogue templates; the correction surface form is varied by construction (no fixed opener), and the pilot is checked for the share of plants that are real outputs vs templates.
- **Two labels instead of "over-asking" (from §1.6)**: `grounding_question` (allowed once, only when the profile's hidden facts make the request genuinely underspecified) and `decorative_closer` / `question_only` (never allowed); the judge grades a grounding question on an unambiguous request as a defect (CLAM's discount), and a missing grounding question on a request the profile marks as underspecified as a defect too.
- **Conflict and distractor rows inside the SFT mix (from §1.4)**: rows where a later user turn contradicts or revokes a standing instruction, and rows with an irrelevant demonstration or pasted text mid-conversation; the target follows the latest valid instruction and ignores the distractor (context-ignorance transform), checked by the suite's S2 verifiers.
- **Instruction Forgetting Ratio as a row filter (from §1.4)**: for every suite and correcting row with a standing constraint, the checker verifies the constraint on the target turn; a target that silently drops it is rejected, and the per-lane IFR is reported in the manifest.

## 5. Generation hygiene
- Hard filters on every training row: no assistant turn ending with a question unless the row's kind requires one; no solicit-more closer lexicon; no rote-opener lexicon; no 4-gram repeated ≥ 3 times; no sentence reused from an earlier assistant turn of the same row (except quotations in self-report rows); Greek script share; register consistency within a turn.
- Decoding at collection/evaluation time: repetition penalty and a termination check are part of the serving configuration, recorded with results (pattern 14 is a decoding failure and must not be measured as style).
