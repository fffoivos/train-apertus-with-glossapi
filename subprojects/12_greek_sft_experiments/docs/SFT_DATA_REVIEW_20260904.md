# SFT data review for the Greek Apertus-8B post-training, 2026-09-04

Scope: what open 3B–30B models disclose about their SFT mixes, whether the survey supports each dataset on our candidate list, the card-level facts for the supported ones, and the literature on four questions that decide the mix (cross-lingual transfer of instruction-following data, how many constraint rows move IFEval, the IFEval gain from preference/RL stages at 7–9B, and forgetting of a continued-pretraining language under English-heavy SFT). Every fact carries a URL to the paper, report, or Hugging Face card it was read from. Where a report does not disclose a number, the table says so.

Reading method: arXiv HTML or PDF text for the papers (Tulu 3, OLMo 3, Apertus, EuroLLM-9B and Krikri PDFs were downloaded and grepped directly), Hugging Face cards for datasets and models. No cluster or GPU commands were run.

Context for the numbers below: our round one used ~20k Greek rows and reached 53–56 % on the ILSP Greek IFEval and 0.40 on Greek MGSM; the owner's harness gives Apertus-8B-Instruct 56 % / 0.53, Llama-Krikri-8B-Instruct 66.8 % / 0.68, Qwen3.5-9B 69.4 % / 0.88, Gemma-3-12B-it 71.9 % / 0.91 on the same two evals.

Other 2026 reports checked and set aside: ZAYA1-8B is a 700M-active MoE trained mostly by an RL cascade ([arXiv 2605.05365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05365)); Solar Open is 102B ([arXiv 2601.07022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07022)); Qwen3.5 has no report.

Two recurring facts in the table. First, every fully-open model in the 7–9B class that reaches 80+ on English IFEval (Tulu 3, OLMo 3) has a dedicated verified constraint-following subset in SFT, adds IF prompts again in DPO, and then runs RL with an IFEval-style verifier; models with no or only a ~1 % IF set (EuroLLM-9B-Instruct 65.7, AMALIA-9B SFT 56.7, Krikri 60.8 on the leaderboard harness) sit 15–25 points lower, although on different harnesses. Second, the three European models built on multilingual SFT (Apertus, EuroLLM, SmolLM3) show a 1–3 point English-to-Multi-IFEval gap, while Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with 3.01 % multilingual SFT shows a 7-point gap on Multi-IFEval and a 29-point gap on Greek IFEval (78.6 vs 71.3 in [Apertus Table 19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14233); 75.1 vs 45.8 in [Krikri Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13772)).

## Part 4. What the literature says

### (a) Does English instruction-following data transfer to other languages?

Partly, and the residual gap is large at 7–8B unless some in-language data is present.

- Chirkova and Nikoulina tuned LLaMA-2 7B/13B and TowerBase-7B on English Dolly (15k) or LIMA (1k) and evaluated in French, Portuguese and Russian. Responses were in the right language and fluent ("between 1.8 and 1.9" on a 0–2 scale), but helpfulness was 1.77 in English vs 1.35 non-English and factuality 1.80 vs 1.46 (human ratings). Transfer required a learning rate of 1e-5 and "large enough" data; small instruction sets "substantially reduce the cross-lingual capabilities" ([arXiv 2402.14778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14778)).
- Shaham et al. (PaLM 2-S, 4,640 examples from LIMA and OpenAssistant, 12 languages, PaLM 2-L judge validated against humans at 75–79.5 % agreement) found that "replacing even just 40 English training examples with multilingual examples significantly improves instruction-following in those languages", and that 2–4 languages in the set generalize best to unseen ones ([arXiv 2401.01854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01854)).
- The Post-trainer's Guide (Aya 23 7B/35B and Llama 3.1 8B, 10k English samples plus 0–5k per language in es/fr/ja/zh, evaluated also on unseen ar/ko/pt) reports that at 7B "IF and summarization results plateau after adding as little as 400 non-English samples per language", while math reasoning needs "roughly 13× more"; adding multilingual data reduced the English–non-English gap by 6.8 % on instruction following at 7B; automatic IF ratings stayed at 3.64–3.88 non-English vs 4.19 English ([arXiv 2504.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16677)).
- "English is Not All You Need" (Qwen3 0.6B/1.7B/8B, Gemma 3 1B/4B, 11 languages, 220 SFT runs on math reasoning and API calling, 10,000 parallel examples per language): adding a single non-English language gave median gains of 9.27 % (API calling) and 8.4 % (math) on that language, +3.38 % / +1.6 % on unseen languages, and improved English itself by 0.88 % / 3.4 %; bilingual beat English-only in 87.5 % of configurations; high-resource languages "plateau rather than degrade" ([arXiv 2604.13286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13286)).
- Marco-Bench-MIF (30 languages including Greek) measures the outcome: top models score 75–85 % on de/fr/zh/ja and 50–60 % on yo/ne/kk; machine-translated test data "underestimates accuracy by 7–22 % versus localized data"; among 7–9B models Qwen2.5-7B averages 55.72 %, Gemma2-9B 63.14 %, EuroLLM-9B 45.77 % ([arXiv 2507.11882](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.11882)).
- The model reports give the Greek-specific picture. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, whose SFT is 3.01 % multilingual, scores 75.1 English vs 45.8 Greek IFEval on ILSP's harness (gap 29.3) and 78.6 vs 71.3 Multi-IFEval (fr/ja/es) on Apertus's harness; Krikri, with 43 % Greek SFT rows, scores 82.4 vs 67.5 (gap 14.9); Apertus-8B-Instruct, with 34 % multilingual plus 23 % regional SFT rows, scores 71.7 vs 68.9 Multi-IFEval (gap 2.8); Qwen3-8B 86.5 vs 82.8; gemma-3-12b-it 80.0 vs 80.2 ([Krikri Table 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13772); [Apertus Table 19](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14233)). Our own harness puts Apertus-8B-Instruct at 56 % on Greek IFEval, which is a 16-point drop from its English score; the Greek IFEval gap is consistently 2–5× the Multi-IFEval gap for the same model.

Reading for our case: an English-heavy stage 1 will raise Greek IFEval, because the constraint skill transfers, but the transferred score sits 15–30 points under English until Greek constraint rows are present; a few hundred to a few thousand Greek IF rows are enough to close most of the gap at this scale, and machine-translated Greek constraint prompts should be localized (units, alphabets, word counts) rather than translated literally.

### (b) How many constraint-following rows move IFEval?

The measured effect saturates early and is driven by verification, not volume.

Three points follow. A 30K verified set in a ~1M mix produces a 12–35 point jump from a base without IF data, and no report shows a second jump from going to 100K+ rows in SFT; Ai2's own progression (30K → 137K between Tulu 3 and Dolci) coincides with a 72.8 → 81.7 rise but confounds data size with QwQ-generated, verifier-filtered responses and a warm start from the thinking model. Unverified or RM-ranked IF data is weaker: Tulu 3's IF-augmented-verified preferences improved IFEval "only by 1 point" while the persona-IF preferences "significantly improve IFEval scores" ([paper, 'Targeting IF' paragraph](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15124)). The remaining gain after SFT comes from preference and RL stages that reuse the constraint prompts with verifiers, not from more SFT rows.

### (d) Forgetting of the continued-pretraining language under English-heavy SFT, and replay

Direct measurements of "Greek-CPT model, then English-only SFT, then Greek IFEval" do not exist in the surveyed literature. The nearest evidence, in decreasing proximity:

- SFT-stage evidence. The two-phase continual instruction fine-tuning study (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3-8B, 11 target languages) finds that fine-tuning on non-English-only data hurts English, that dissimilar consecutive datasets cost "8 % decline for IFEval", and that English-first then multilingual (0.355 mean score) beats a single mixed run (0.325) when the two datasets are similar ([arXiv 2410.16006](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16006)). Forgetting at the SFT stage is symmetric: the same mechanism that erodes English under target-only SFT erodes the target under English-only SFT, which is why every adapted model below keeps both languages in every stage.
- Adapted-model practice. Krikri keeps Greek at 43.3 % of stage-1 SFT rows (371,379 of 856,946), 43.8 % of stage 2, and 51 % of DPO triplets, and retains English within +0.4 points of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on the leaderboard average while gaining 21.7 points on Greek IFEval ([card](https://huggingface.co/ilsp/Llama-Krikri-8B-Instruct); [Table 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13772)). BgGPT (Gemma 2 base) uses 100,000 English rows (SlimOrca, MetaMath), ~31,000 translated Bulgarian rows and 140,000 Bulgarian user conversations in instruction tuning and merges the Bulgarian and English models with a SLERP ratio searched between 0.3 and 0.7 ([arXiv 2412.10893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10893)). The Arabic bilingual adaptation of Llama 2 fine-tunes on "approximately 10 million English and 4 million Arabic examples" after finding 1:9 English:Arabic sufficient in CPT ([arXiv 2407.12869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12869)). Swallow (Japanese) trains SFT in two stages, code-only first and then code plus Japanese dialogue, because mixing them in one stage "reduced the effectiveness of the code generation SFT data" ([Swallow](https://swallow-llm.github.io/llama3.3-swallow.en.html); [arXiv 2404.17790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17790)). Sailor2 runs SFT on 4.8M rows in which English is 34.8 % and applies a language-consistency verifier to preference data ([arXiv 2502.12982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12982)). Luciole-8B is the one counter-example: ~30 % French base, post-training "almost entirely on English data", and a claim of strong French instruction following, but no numbers in text ([card](https://huggingface.co/OpenLLM-France/Luciole-8B-Instruct-1.1)).
- Replay ratios (continued pretraining, not SFT). Ibrahim et al. test 0–50 % replay at 405M and 10B: 1 % already "significantly reduces forgetting" on a weak shift and 5 % is chosen; the strong shift (English → German) "requires more replay" and 25 % is recommended ([arXiv 2403.08763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08763)). The cross-lingual CPT study (50M–5.5B, English → Chinese/French/Russian) concludes "5 % – 30 % of source language replaying effectively prevents forgetting" ([arXiv 2407.02118](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.02118)). Swallow uses 9:1 Japanese:English ([arXiv 2404.17790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17790)); the Arabic study uses 1:9 English:Arabic ([arXiv 2407.12869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12869)); Krikri interleaves "small replay segments of high-quality English or code" ([§3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13772)). The mirror case, adapting an instruct model to a new language with full fine-tuning, loses 20.3 % (7B) and 22.3 % (13B) on English source tasks on OLMo 2 Instruct without mitigation ([ACL 2026, Yamaguchi et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.865.pdf)).
- Minimum in-language dose. The two transfer studies above give the floor: 40 examples changes behavior at PaLM 2-S scale ([arXiv 2401.01854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01854)), and instruction following plateaus at ~400 rows per language at 7B when the base is 10k English rows, i.e. ~4 % ([arXiv 2504.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16677)).

Reading for our case: there is no published run where a CPT language survives a multi-million-row English-only SFT untouched, and the adapted models that report Greek/Bulgarian/Japanese/Arabic retention all keep 30–50 % in-language rows in SFT or run a two-stage recipe. A stage 1 that is English-heavy is defensible only if Greek is present at a few percent throughout (the 4 % plateau from the Post-trainer's Guide is the lowest tested that works at 7B), and the Greek stage 2 needs English replay in the 10–25 % band the CPT literature uses for strong shifts, which the current plan's 10 % is at the low end of.

Sizes refer to the plan's tier M (about 1M rows for stage 1). Percentages are of stage 1.

Adds:

1. allenai/Dolci-Instruct-DPO (259,922 pairs, ODC-BY) becomes the primary DPO source. It is the only preference set with a published 7B result on the same prompt distribution as the SFT backbone and it already carries 41,724 precise-IF prompts ([Table 30](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13961)).
2. Greek rows inside stage 1, not only in stage 2: 40–50K of our adapted Greek rows (4–5 %) mixed uniformly through stage 1, following the ~400-rows-per-language plateau at 7B ([arXiv 2504.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16677)) and the 40-example threshold ([arXiv 2401.01854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01854)); Krikri's 43 % is the upper reference ([card](https://huggingface.co/ilsp/Llama-Krikri-8B-Instruct)).
3.
