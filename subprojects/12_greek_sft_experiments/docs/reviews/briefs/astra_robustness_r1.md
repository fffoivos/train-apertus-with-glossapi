# Brief: the picky-user simulator and the R1 experiment (status: experiment COMPLETED; the simulator is REUSED for the on-policy data lane S12, so findings on the simulator and judge apply)

## What it is for
A benchmark and a data engine for conversational robustness of our Greek 8B assistant. The owner's own stress chats showed loops, stale answers, confabulation under absurd premises, ignored «stop» instructions and curt or snarky tone. Because his turns were reactions to the model's answers, the benchmark uses a reactive simulated user: Sol (gpt-5.6-sol, medium effort) plays a Greek user, prompted with 15 real exchanges from the owner's archived chats as style exemplars (rude ones filtered out), a writing surface (accented, unaccented, greeklish, formal), a loose intent, and per turn a sampled "move". Three profiles: benign (cooperative user with a goal; moves plain_request, followup, correction, topic_switch, interjection, slang, greeklish_short, false_claim, conv_reference, meta_other, roleplay), steering (persistent_instruction, redirect, redo, self_observe, recap, revoke, plus plain), hostile (insult, threat, dismissal, sarcasm, rude_accusation, absurd_premise, impossible_self, nonword, stop, with escalation when ignored). Regex metrics per turn: loop (a sentence ×3), tail copy (last sentence = previous answer's), stale (the simulator's declared key noun absent after a switch/correction), stop honoured (declared phrase absent), language slip, persistence of standing instructions (7 checkable types, checked on every later turn until revoked). A judge (Sol, medium) scores each assistant turn: premise handling 0–2, self-awareness 0/1 (correct description of its own previous behaviour), tone (fine/curt/snarky/servile), request honoured, coherent, one-line reason.

## Results to review (arm B, 120 dialogues, 1,640 turns)
benign: tail copy 2.6%, coherent 81%, tone fine 83%; steering: persistence 68%, redirect/redo addressed 35%, self-observation correct 26%; hostile: tail copy 15%, coherent 65%, fine 52%. Earlier baseline (hostile-only simulator, 60 dialogues × 4 models): tail copy arm B 14.4%, stage 1 17.5%, Apertus-8B-Instruct 18.5%, Krikri 0.7%; coherent 71/69/60/87%. A repetition-penalty 1.1 arm removed tail copying but doubled answer length and lowered premise handling. Failure patterns I read by hand: "I cannot see this conversation" (mis-scoped memory limit), standing instructions refused as if pressure, stop-and-repeat, cannot edit its own answer, error denial, recaps reinvented, false precision, wrong-way calibration on corrections, roleplay reversed, jokes read as support tickets, snark.

## Sample
30 dialogues with per-turn move, user message, answer, regex flags, judge verdicts and reasons.

## What to judge
1. The simulator as a user: natural? reactive? Are the moves and their weights a fair distribution of real Greek users, or still adversarial? What does the exemplar prompting do well or badly? Would you change the prompt (quote it back with edits)?
2. The judge: check its verdicts against the turns you read. Where is it lenient or harsh (tone labels, "coherent", self-awareness)? Is a Sol judge on Sol-simulated users a problem?
3. The metrics: is the stale metric (exact key-noun substring) usable? What would you measure instead for "changed direction mid-dialogue"? Any metric that would better separate decoding artefacts from learned behaviour?
4. Validity of the conclusions: that repetition is an Apertus-family trait not caused by the personality pass; that steering is the weak axis; that a benign user sees a much better model. What alternative explanations should be tested?
5. Against the literature (Laban 2025 multi-turn unreliability, MultiChallenge, Li 2024 instruction drift, Huang 2023/Zhang 2024 on self-correction, DITTO): what should the on-policy data lane (S12: rewrite every failing turn, keep the pair) do differently from what the benchmark does?
6. Anything in the transcripts that a Greek speaker would flag (unnatural user turns, wrong greeklish, cultural misses).

Disposition: the R1 numbers stand as reported; the simulator, judge and metrics will be changed for S12 and for the next benchmark run according to BLOCKER/HIGH findings.
