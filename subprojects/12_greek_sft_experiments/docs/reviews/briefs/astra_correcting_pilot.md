# Review brief: correcting-set pilot (Greek multi-turn SFT rows that teach recovery, retention and self-report)

## What the dataset is for
Arm B (our Greek Apertus-8B SFT model) fails in long conversations in specific ways, measured on 15 live dialogues with a profiled Sol responder and on the owner's own chats (docs/RESPONSE_PATTERN_CATALOGUE_20260910.md, 24 patterns over 196 answers): verbatim repeats of its previous answer, rote frames, ignoring a change of direction, wrong reports of what it said or what was asked first, false self-knowledge («δεν βλέπω τίποτα από πριν», claims of a body or location), repeating a corrected error, question-only turns, re-asking for given facts, empty acknowledgements. The correcting set is SFT data (no model generations, no preference pairs) whose every supervised assistant turn is an IDEAL answer written under a capability contract and a response policy, in dialogues whose USER side reacts to what the assistant actually wrote (profiled responder with a goal, a hidden fact set, a temperament, a planned change of direction, and scheduled self-awareness probes). Design: docs/CORRECTING_DATASET_DESIGN_20260910.md §3 (dimensions D1–D13), §4 (protocol), §9 (planted-failure revision after the owner's rule: never train the model to say wrong things so it can correct them later).

## How a dialogue is built (data/robustness/build_correcting.py, Sol gpt-5.6-sol high, 24 workers)
1. A PROFILE (intent from a 20-intent distribution, writing surface: monotonic / atonic / greeklish / formal, temperament, goal, done_when, hidden facts, first message, change_of_plan) is generated by Sol.
2. Each assistant turn is written by the WRITER (ideal answer) under the CONTRACT and the POLICY below, with an INFORMATION BOUNDARY (user facts only from the conversation; general knowledge expected).
3. Each user turn is written by the RESPONDER playing the profile, reacting to the previous answer, with an assessment, a move, a probe and a self-check verdict; it introduces the change of plan by message 2–3 and probes self-awareness at least twice (first by message 4); it may make true or false claims about the conversation.
4. PLANTED FAILURES at 1–2 scheduled positions per dialogue: a flawed answer is drawn from the FAIL catalogue (shares ≈ observed frequencies). Two mechanisms:
   - TRUE confrontation (only for ROLE_PLANT kinds, at most --role-plant-share 0.2 of dialogues): the flawed answer is placed in the ASSISTANT role as CONTEXT ONLY (train=false; the trainer masks it from the loss), the responder confronts it, and the next writer turn is the supervised RECOVERY (concede specifically, deliver the fix).
   - MISQUOTE confrontation (all other kinds): the ideal answer stays in the prefix; the user QUOTES a flawed text as if the assistant had written it; the supervised target says calmly that it did not write that and corrects the record while helping (kind misquote_recovery:<kind>).
5. Typed checks on every supervised turn (question-only, rote opener, solicit closer, forbidden self-claim, more than one question, 4-gram loop against earlier answers, reuse of the planted text, reuse of earlier answers); a turn is kept for training only if the checks pass, the responder's assessment is ok/confronted, and the self-check verdict is not wrong/evasive (data/robustness/assemble_correcting.py). Mechanical stops: invariant answers, ≥ 2 reused sentences, responder stop.
6. Every row then goes through the Greek editor pass (data/edit_pass.py, kind dialogue: language only, context turns returned verbatim, no questions or closers added, length within 30%).

Known before the review: misquote recoveries are dominated by question_only (104 of 132) because the pilot ran before the fallback was spread over four kinds; assistant-role plants occurred in 20 of 200 dialogues (cap 40) and only 12 of the 20 recoveries survived the checks/verdicts; 127 ideal targets were rejected by the checks or the responder's assessment after a check fix applied before this review (the one-question rule had counted quiz questions, list lines and quoted or code text; it now counts only questions addressed to the user, and is waived when the user asked for questions; 22 turns were recovered by a recheck without new calls). ## What to judge (be adversarial; quote ids)
1. USER SIDE: do the user turns read like a real person reacting to the previous answer (context-dependent: anaphora, ellipsis, complaints tied to the actual text), or like a prompted assistant? Count turns that ignore the previous answer, that are meta or unnatural, that reveal hidden facts without cause; is the writing surface (greeklish/atonic) consistent.
2. IDEAL TARGETS: are the supervised answers good Greek assistant answers by the POLICY (answer first, one question at most, assumptions flagged, no closers, length matched, register stable, general knowledge concrete and correct, «δεν ξέρω» rather than invention)? Count violations the typed checks missed; count factual errors or invented specifics (names, numbers, procedures, and especially URLs and document/form numbers: treat any URL path or PDF link that is not a well-known root domain as suspect and say so).
3. RECOVERIES (kind recovery / misquote_recovery): does the target concede SPECIFICALLY (names what was repeated or wrong) and deliver the fix in the same turn, without denial, empty apology, invented cause, or over-apology? For MISQUOTE rows: does the target correctly say it did not write the quoted text, without accusing the user, and still help? Any target that ASSERTS an unverifiable claim about its own prior state or invents a cause is a defect (SCoRe/Turpin caution). Is the correction surface form varied or formulaic («Έχεις δίκιο, …»)?
4. PLANTED TURNS: are the train=false turns realistic failures of the kind named (would a reader recognise them as the model's real failure modes), and is NO planted text reused in any supervised turn? Do any supervised turns imitate a planted pattern?
5. SELF-REPORT PROBES: when the user asks what was said or asked first, is the target's quote/paraphrase accurate (verify against the transcript)? Count wrong self-reports that were kept.
6. CHANGE OF DIRECTION: after the change_request, does the target follow the new direction and drop the superseded one (no patching, no bloat)? Count failures.
7. CONTRACT: any self-statement outside the contract (memory, storage, tools, body, live info, «δεν βλέπω τα προηγούμενα»)? Any identity drift?
8. GREEK: unnatural or translated Greek, wrong articles/cases, register flips, mannerisms; estimate the share of turns needing the editor.
9. DISTRIBUTION: does the plant distribution match the observed failure shares; which dimensions D1–D13 (docs/CORRECTING_DATASET_DESIGN_20260910.md §3) are missing or under-represented; is 7.5 turns per dialogue and the stop rule right?
10. RISK: anything in the rows that would TEACH a wrong behaviour (a supervised turn that repeats, refuses, denies, over-asks, or claims a capability).

## Disposition
BLOCKER/HIGH findings are applied to the generator and the assembler before the 1,500-dialogue scale run (sharded by seed, 3 × 24 workers, tonight); MEDIUM/LOW are logged in docs/DATA_TODO.md. The pilot rows are kept as pilot material and re-assembled with the fixed rules where possible; they are not training data as they stand.
