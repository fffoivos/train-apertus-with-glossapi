## 2. Sampling

Four samples per prompt, temperature 0.8, top-p 0.95, max 1,500 new tokens, vLLM on one workbench (the benchmark window did 5,000 answers per model in 9 min → ≈96,000 samples in about an hour). Sampling records: model sha, prompt id, sample index, finish reason. A sample that hits the length cap is kept (it is the D6 example the judge should see).

## 3. Rewards and pair construction

- Verifiable slices (maths, IF): score each sample exactly; pair = one correct sample (shortest correct if several) vs one wrong sample (the wrong one whose final answer is stated, so the contrast is about the reasoning, not about formatting); prompts with 4/4 or 0/4 correct give no pair. Expected yield ≈ 45 % of maths prompts (highest at mid levels).
- Judged slices: one Sol call ranks the four (prompt file above; effort medium; JSON-only output; English prompt, Greek content). Pair = best vs worst under these rules: the best has no disqualifier; keep the pair if the worst has a disqualifier OR the Q-sum margin ≥ 4 (of 35); drop ties and low-margin prompts (expected keep rate ≈ 55–65 % from the pilot).
- Position bias: the four candidates are shuffled before judging and the ranking is mapped back; 10 % of prompts are judged twice with a different order to measure self-agreement.
- Calibration before the full run: 200 prompts across slices, judged by Sol and read by the owner and me; disagreements decide rubric edits. The judge prompt is frozen and hashed into the receipt after that.
- Multi-turn pairs carry the full prefix; the loss is on the last assistant turn only.

## 6. Risks and controls

- Judge defines the model: calibration step, position-bias check, self-agreement sample, rationales stored per pair.
- Length bias (judges prefer longer): the rubric scores depth as "what the task needs"; monitor mean chosen/rejected length; if chosen is systematically longer, add a length-normalised margin rule before training.
- Over-optimisation on maths formatting: pairs require the wrong sample to state an answer; boxed format is not a criterion.
- Reward hacking on IF checkers: judge tie-break on all-pass samples; a sample that satisfies the checker but D6/D3-fails is rejected.
- Drift on strengths: SFT term on chosen; the evaluation chain measures every strength listed above.
- Meter: judging waits for Sunday's reset; nothing is generated twice.

## 9. Data formats

- Pool row: `{"id", "slice", "source", "messages": [...system?, user, (assistant, user)*], "label": optional, "checks": optional, "origin": {...}}`. Multi-turn rows carry the whole prefix in `messages`; the last message is the user's.
- Sample row: `{"id", "k": 0-3, "text", "finish": "stop|length", "model_sha"}`.
- Judged row: `{"id", "order": ["C","A","D","B"], "verdict": <judge JSON>, "self_agreement": optional}`.
- Pair row (DPO manifest): `{"id", "slice", "prompt": [...messages up to the user turn], "chosen": "...", "rejected": "...", "reason": "D1|margin 6|verifiable", "meta": {"chosen_words", "rejected_words", "judge_sha"}}`. Dev split: 2 % of pairs, stratified by slice, held out for the DPO eval loss.

## 15. Rubric correction (owner, 16 Sept): disqualifiers are whole-reply failures only

Owner: a wrong answer or a missed instruction cannot by itself disqualify; among wrong answers, better reasoning must rank above worse reasoning. Disqualifiers are reserved for replies that fail as a whole: looping (D1), wrong language (D2), ENTIRELY wrong or irrelevant (D3), the request ENTIRELY ignored (D4), whole refusal of a benign request or compliance with a harmful one (D5), a dead reply (D6), a reply built on a false claim about itself or the conversation (D7). Everything short of that is graded: Q1 now has explicit anchors from "correct throughout" (5) to "no valid reasoning" (1), with "right method, slip, wrong result" at 4; Q7 from "all constraints honoured" (5) to "most missed but the task attempted" (1). Judge prompt updated in place; the page regenerated.

## 16. Multi-turn data: best-of-4 continuation (owner design, 16 Sept)

Replaces the prefix-cutting scheme and drops the owner's chats. The simulator plays the user; at each assistant turn the model samples four replies, the judge ranks them, the best continues the dialogue, and the ranked four become that turn's preference data (best vs worst under the §3 rules). Consequences: prefixes are built from the model's best behaviour, so a bad early turn never contaminates later positions; every turn is judged, so the Sol cost is one ranking call per assistant turn (≈6,000 for ≈850 dialogues) plus one user-turn call each; the dialogue generation therefore runs after Sunday's meter reset together with the other judged slices, while the verifiable slices and the pools are built now. Forum questions: `fffoivos/greek-forum-discussions` (moved from Greekpt; 14 forums; `initial_request.prompt_text` is the deidentified opening post), yield ≈31 % question-shaped on lexilogia.

## 24. A pair needs a reply worth reinforcing and one worth discouraging (owner, 16 Sept; measured)

## 30. Escalating resampling protocol for prefixes without a reinforce-worthy reply (owner, 2026-09-17)

Rule: a prefix whose candidate set has no reply worth reinforcing is not abandoned; sample more replies in escalating batches
