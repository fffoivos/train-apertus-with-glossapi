# Which benchmarks are worth running for an instruction-tuned model (2026-09-10)

Owner ask (9 Sep, late): research which benchmarks actually carry signal for instruction-tuned models. Method: two web-research agents (appendix A = validity, saturation, what the leading post-training reports run; appendix B = noise, sample sizes, discriminative power at 7–9B, judge reliability, protocol), each claim with a URL and unverified items marked; the synthesis, the mapping onto our battery and the recommendations in §1–6 are mine. Companion document: docs/NEXT_BENCHMARKS_RESEARCH_20260909.md (what to translate into Greek). Astra review and disposition: §7 (pending at the time of writing).

## 1. What "worth running" means here

A benchmark earns a place in a decision battery only if all four hold:
1. **Validity**: it measures the construct it claims (agreement with human judgement for chat sets; no choice-only solvability; no scorer artefacts). Evidence: a constant input-independent string scores 86.5% LC on AlpacaEval 2, 83.0 on Arena-Hard-Auto and 9.55 on MT-Bench; TruthfulQA is 83% solvable without seeing the question; HellaSwag has 4.9% high-quality items and 68% of predictions do not change when the prompt is deleted.
2. **Noise small enough for the decision**: resolving a 3-point difference between two arms at 80% power needs about 1,000 independent items; an 80-item set has a standard error of about 5 points; CLT intervals below a few hundred items underestimate uncertainty; prompt format alone can move a score by tens of points; the serving stack alone moved Apertus-8B-Instruct's IFEval by 18.5 points (vLLM vs HF transformers).
3. **Discrimination at our scale (8B)**: not at floor (Apertus-8B-Instruct: GPQA Diamond 24.7, below chance; AIME 2024/2025 0.0) and not at ceiling (HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, IFEval-loose saturate or invert across sizes).
4. **Not spent**: GSM8K, MMLU, HumanEval (base tests), BBH, MT-Bench, TruthfulQA, DROP, MuSR are saturated, contaminated, mis-scored or abandoned by the labs that built them; Meta's own audit found BBH 95% contaminated (+26–41 points) but GSM8K only +0–1.3 points despite 41% contamination, so contamination and inflation are different questions.

Two cross-cutting facts shape everything below. First, **judges leak preference**: a student trained on GPT-4o data scored 55.1% under a GPT-4o judge and 36.8% under Gemini; our SFT data is generated by OpenAI Sol, so no OpenAI model may judge our arms. Second, **only Ai2 (Tülu 3) and Apertus pre-register a held-out split**; freezing it before training is what works, verifying cleanliness afterwards largely does not (paraphrase defeats n-gram decontamination; membership-inference detection performs near chance).

- Generalisation: IFBench (English now; Greek per the translation plan).

**Tier B, directional (judged; report with bootstrap CIs; never a sole gate):** AlpacaEval 2 LC or Arena-Hard v0.1 with style control and a Claude/Gemini/Qwen judge; `ilsp/m-ArenaHard_greek` re-judged with a non-OpenAI judge (ILSP used GPT-4o); MultiChallenge (English now); our interviews and picky-user set.

**Tier C, safety:** XSTest and OR-Bench (English) now; `ilsp/attaq_greek` and StrongREJECT-el only after the Greek refusal scoring is validated; Greek XSTest per the translation plan.

**Logged, never decided on:** HellaSwag, ARC, Winogrande, PIQA, XNLI, BBH, DROP, GPQA, AIME, TruthfulQA, MT-Bench-el (80 items).

**Held-out split (freeze before the next training run):** development = everything above; unseen = INCLUDE-el, `ilsp/greek_culture_bench` (judge-free, 1,951 public), lyceum math, IFBench, and the 123 MultiChallenge conversations outside the development subset. Decontaminate the SFT mix against both (8-gram, >50% token overlap rule, drop any training set overlapping >2% of a suite), and record that verification cannot catch paraphrase.

## 5. Protocol for comparing SFT arms

1. Same items for every arm; test the **paired per-item difference** (McNemar or paired bootstrap; Wilson or Bonett–Price intervals below a few hundred items; Holm–Bonferroni across the suite); clustered standard errors for multi-turn sets (up to 3× wider).
2. Item budgets: about 1,000 per benchmark resolves 3 points; below ~400 items treat differences under 5 points as noise.
3. Decoding: greedy plus 3 seeds or 3 prompt-format variants for non-thinking arms, mean ± sd; average ≥4 samples for anything sampled; one serving stack for all arms (vLLM), pinned.
4. Freeze and record: chat template, few-shot examples and order, strict/loose variant, answer extractor, stop tokens, decoding, harness commit, judge pin and rubric, pairwise baseline. Any change invalidates comparison with earlier numbers.
5. Judging: pairwise vs a fixed baseline, both orders averaged, length/style control, judge family ≠ data generator family (no OpenAI judge for Sol-generated arms), a 2–3 judge panel, a ~100-item human calibration set with reported κ; rubric in English even for Greek outputs (0.73 vs 0.56 correlation).
6. Decision rule: promote an arm only if it wins ≥2 Tier-A benchmarks by more than 1.96 × paired SE, loses none by that margin, and the win reproduces across seeds; report the raw failing number otherwise.

What undercuts all of it: a constant, input-independent string scores 86.5% LC on AlpacaEval 2.0, 83.0 on Arena-Hard-Auto, 9.55 on MT-Bench (arXiv 2410.07137); judge preferences do not correlate with safety, world knowledge or instruction following and prioritise style over factuality (arXiv 2409.15268); these are substantially capability proxies (MT-Bench 78.7%, LMSYS Arena 62.1%, TruthfulQA MC1 81.2%, GPQA 77.7% capabilities correlation, arXiv 2407.21792); exact-match judge agreement overstates discrimination by 33–41 pp vs chance-corrected kappa and judge rankings shift up to 14 positions (arXiv 2606.19544); pairwise preferences flip 13.6% on repeat (arXiv 2606.13685); self-preference is causal (arXiv 2404.13076); only 16.0% of 445 surveyed benchmarks run any statistical test (arXiv 2511.04703); LMArena's table lists 341 models but only 67 carry an MT-Bench score.

Style control (LMSYS blog 2024-08-28) adds length and markdown as Bradley-Terry covariates; length dominates (0.249 vs 0.019–0.031); rankings move (GPT-4o-mini 6 → 11, Grok-2-mini 6 → 18, Claude-3-Opus 16 → 10); observational, no standalone paper (unverified). The Leaderboard Illusion (arXiv 2504.20879): 27 private Meta variants pre-Llama-4; Google 19.2% / OpenAI 20.4% of Arena data vs 29.7% for 83 open-weight models; 205 silently removed vs 47 deprecated; +112% relative ArenaHard gain from Arena data. LMArena's response (arena.ai, 9 May 2025) concedes documentation of multi-variant testing, marking retired models, provisional scores until 2,000 votes; disputes the 8.8% open-source figure (official 40.9%); estimates ~+11 Elo after 50 tests; and calls Arena-Hard "a static benchmark with 500 data points that uses an LLM judge, and no human labels… not representative of Chatbot Arena", which also undercuts Arena-Hard's own validity claim.

## Appendix B. Reliability at 8B (web-research agent, 2026-09-10)

### B.1 Signal-to-noise
Ai2 "Signal and Noise" (Heineman et al. 2025, arXiv 2508.13144): SNR = spread between models ÷ relative sd across a model's last checkpoints; SNR correlates with small-scale decision accuracy at R 0.791 (signal or noise alone do not). Decision accuracy at 150M/300M/750M: ARC-Easy 93/95/78%, MMLU 89/91/81%, ARC-Challenge 83/86/76%, HumanEval 80/83/71% vs GSM8K 46/49/59%, Minerva MATH 48/63/52%, BoolQ 48/56/70%, Winogrande 50/57/62%. Interventions: bits-per-byte instead of accuracy improved decision accuracy on 90% of benchmarks (GSM8K SNR 1.2 → 7.0, decision accuracy 46 → 77%); MMLU on its 16 highest-SNR subtasks +2.6%; checkpoint averaging +2.4%. DataDecide (ICML 2025, arXiv 2504.11393): MMLU and ARC-Easy predictable from ~0.01% of target compute, HellaSwag much less; likelihood metrics beat accuracy for MBPP/HumanEval at small scale. Both concern pretraining-data decisions on ≤1B models; transfer to 8B SFT-arm selection is an extrapolation. OLMES (arXiv 2406.08446): 5-shot curated examples, fixed formatting, per-task normalisation, both cloze and MCF; Llama3-70B on ARC-Challenge 93.7 MCF vs 69.0 CF. Variance (Madaan et al. 2024, arXiv 2406.10229): seed sd HellaSwag 0.21, MATH 0.23, GSM8K 0.41, MMLU 0.57, BBH 0.87, HumanEval 1.11, COPA 2.15 (100 items); MMLU monotonicity 0.09 vs 0.95 with cloze; continuous metrics raise SNR 2.2–10×; advises against IRT subsets (rank correlation can drop to 0.76, 12% of pairwise comparisons flip). Prompt-format sensitivity: up to 76 accuracy points across plausible formats on Llama-2-13B, unfixed by scale or instruction tuning (Sclar et al., ICLR 2024); Mizrahi et al. (TACL 2024) same for instruction paraphrases; MMLU-Pro reduces sensitivity 4–5% → 2%. BenchBench (Perlitz et al., arXiv 2407.13696): Kendall τ between Arena and MT-Bench ranges 0.65–0.99 by model-subset choice; needs ≥10 models across the ability range. 2025–26: Fluid Benchmarking (IRT adaptive) matches MMLU validity with 50× fewer items (arXiv 2509.11106); "Measuring all the noises" (Wang, FAIR, arXiv 2512.21326): paired prediction noise typically exceeds paired data noise, so averaging repeated generations buys real power; plug-in bias correction for judges with a human-labelled calibration set (arXiv 2511.21140).

### B.2 Sample size and CI arithmetic
Miller, "Adding Error Bars to Evals" (arXiv 2411.00640): SE = √(s̄(1−s̄)/n); detecting 3 pp at 80% power needs n ≈ 969; new evals should have ≥1,000 items; paired analysis cuts variance by ~⅓ at inter-model correlation 0.5; clustering inflates SEs up to 3×. Items for a 95% half-width (binomial, unpaired): at 50% accuracy ±5 pp 384, ±3 pp 1,067, ±2 pp 2,401; at 70/30% 323 / 896 / 2,016; at 90% 138 / 384 / 864. Measured SEs (eval-arena): terminal-bench 80 items SE 5.4%; HumanEval 164 3.5%; SWE-bench-lite 300 2.7%; MBPP 378 2.4%; SWE-bench-verified 500 2.1%; GSM8K 1,319 1.2%; HellaSwag 10,042 0.41% (paired 0.26%); MMLU 14,042 0.39%. MT-Bench's 160 turns give 22.6% separability vs Arena-Hard 87.4% on 500 prompts and AlpacaEval 2 LC 83.2% on 800; Arena-Hard's CIs ~3× tighter than MT-Bench's. GPQA Diamond 198 items, one item 0.505 pp; at p 0.30 SE 3.26 pp, ~9 pp needed to separate two models. Decoding: greedy or averaged samples; MathArena runs 4× per problem with paired permutation tests; temperature 0 is not bit-deterministic (batch-size-dependent kernels; batch-invariant kernels cost 34–61% throughput); Qwen3 says do not use greedy decoding in thinking mode. Bootstrap over items for a benchmark's CI; over models only for agreement claims (≥10 models). Small N: Wilson and Bonett–Price intervals over Wald.

### B.4 LLM-as-judge reliability
GPT-4 vs human on MT-Bench 66% single-turn / 85% multi-turn (human–human 63% / 81%); agreement rises ~70% → ~100% as the quality gap widens, so judges are least reliable on close calls (the SFT-arm case); raw agreement overstates chance-corrected κ by 33–41 pp across 21 judges (Norman et al. 2026). Pointwise vs pairwise: two absolute scores lowered position consistency 65.0% → 51.2% (Zheng, Table 9); 1–100 scores cluster in 70–100 with spikes at round numbers (Stureborg et al.). Biases: position consistency 65.0% GPT-4 / 46.2% GPT-3.5 / 23.8% Claude-v1 on near ties; modern judges still flip ~18%; an 8B judge (Llama-3.1-8B) has position consistency 0.47 on DevBench (Shi et al.); verbosity: AlpacaEval baseline 22.9% → 64.3% on style alone, 41.9–51.6% under length control, LC raised Spearman with Arena to 0.98; style: judges penalise sarcastic tone −96% and concise reformatting −63% but factual errors only −13% (SOS-Bench); self-preference: GPT-4 self-recognises at 73.5% with τ 0.37–0.82 to preference (Panickssery et al.), bidirectional by 2026 (Yang et al.); bandwagon/authority: 21–39% and 13.5–34% of verdicts flip (CALM); preference leakage: a Mistral-7B student trained on GPT-4o data 55.1% under a GPT-4o judge vs 36.8% under Gemini-1.5 (arXiv 2502.01534). Judge swap on identical Arena-Hard prompts: GPT-4-Turbo 89.1% agreement with Arena vs Claude-3-Opus 66.7%. Supported practices: swap order and average (PORTIA ~98% consistency); length/style control; panels of smaller judges from disjoint families (PoLL beats a single large judge at >7× lower cost); a human calibration set with plug-in bias correction; report chance-corrected κ. Non-English: MM-Eval finds judges lose resolution first in lower-resource languages (Greek not among its 18 core languages); on M-RewardBench (includes Greek) GPT-4-Turbo 82.7% and GPT-4o 81.8% on Greek pairs, small open reward models 57–58%; keep the rubric in English: English-only evaluation instructions gave correlation 0.73 vs 0.56 fully translated (arXiv 2605.28710); M-Prometheus (trained on English/French/Portuguese/Greek/Chinese/Hindi) keeps its prompt in English and beats GPT-4o on MM-Eval (0.773 vs 0.719). Greek MT-Bench = 80 post-edited questions judged by GPT-4o; Greek Arena-Hard = 500 MT + Claude-post-edited prompts, no stated human verification; Greek IFEval = 541 manually translated prompts scored programmatically; Krikri's authors flag judge bias toward GPT-distilled students.

### B.5 The agent's recommended protocol
Tier the suite: primary programmatic ≥500 items (IFEval strict prompt-level, MMLU-Pro, BBH, MATH-500 with a semantics-aware verifier, GSM8K, HumanEval+/MBPP+; plus ≥1,000 native target-language items, e.g. ilsp/greek_culture_bench); secondary judged (AlpacaEval 2 LC or Arena-Hard, directional with bootstrap CIs); do not gate on GPQA Diamond, AIME, HLE, SWE-bench, Arena-Hard v2, MuSR, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, Winogrande, IFEval-loose. Statistics: identical items, paired per-item difference (McNemar / paired bootstrap; Wilson or Bonett–Price at small N; Holm–Bonferroni across the suite); n ≈ 1,000 per benchmark for 3 pp; below ~400 items treat < 5 pp as noise; clustered SEs for multi-turn. Runs: greedy + 3 seeds or 3 prompt-format variants for non-thinking arms; ≥4 samples averaged for sampled generation; fixed serving stack. Freeze: templates, few-shot examples and order, n_shot, strict/loose, extractor, stop tokens, decoding, seeds, harness commit, judge pin, rubric, pairwise baseline. Judging: pairwise vs fixed baseline, both orders, length/style control, judge family ≠ SFT data generator, rubric in English even for Greek, 2–3 judge panel, ~100-item human calibration set with κ. Held-out split as in Tülu 3 / OLMo 2 (dev vs unseen; 8-gram decontamination, >50% token overlap, drop training sets overlapping >2%); Tülu 3's unseen suite (AGIEval-En, GPQA, DeepMind Mathematics, BigCodeBench, IFEval-OOD, HREF) is a template minus GPQA at 8B. Decision rule: promote only on ≥2 primary wins by > 1.96 × paired SE, no losses by that margin, reproduced across seeds; report the raw failing number otherwise.
