## 1. Questions and decisions
Primary question: where should preference-training examples be drawn from an actual Apertus conversation?
Compare three sources of signal:
- Prevention: alternatives at the prefix immediately before the first serious assistant failure.
- Recovery: alternatives at a later prefix containing a genuine earlier mistake and an actual opportunity to repair it.
- Retention: alternatives at successful turns and in trajectories with no observed serious failure.

First measure quality against depth, then choose prefixes; do not prescribe shallow/deep quotas beforehand.
Do not assume every chat eventually fails. Do not interpret a local preference improvement as demonstrated prevention of future failures.
Output of this investigation is an evidence-backed allocation recommendation and a small reviewed preference pilot, not a newly trained model.

## 5. Phase 1 — smoke, time and calibrate
Run6 smoke trajectories (at most18 Apertus completions); inspect exact template/model identity and receipts.
Sol user-turn generation: independently process several trajectories per batch; no trajectory gets a second follow-up until its actual Apertus response exists.
Check maximum context size; do not silently trim old messages. Context limit, truncated assistant output, task completion, infrastructure failure and planned horizon each have distinct terminal codes.
Preflight source facts are visible only where the user would legitimately know them. Sol simulator never sees future answer candidates or judge ratings.
Annotate every smoke response. Root reviews all18 or fewer; verify factual/constraint issues against references. Ask for owner calibration only for unresolved preference choices, not technical correctness or routine progress.
Measure tokens, wall time, throughput, billed provider setup/runtime, and Sol call consumption. Forecast full panel costs with conservative observed rates and a separate judging/candidate reserve.
Gate: zero unresolved plumbing errors; costed admitted size. If semantics/rubric needs changes, rerun only affected smoke cases and version the change; exclude all smoke from measurement estimates.

## 6. Phase 2 — collect raw trajectories
Freeze the admitted manifest, interaction policy, inference settings and ending policy.
Generate in depth waves across trajectories; batch target inference and Sol user prompts where practical. Raw Apertus n=1 always.
Keep full planned trajectories despite serious failures. Do not let the annotation process steer user prompts or truncate difficult cases.
User simulator may acknowledge a clear mistake it can identify from the visible task, as an ordinary user would; distinguish that from externally injected corrections. Keep the policy stable and report its identity with estimates.
Continue genuine user tasks, not artificial padding to force eight turns. Design seeds with plausible multi-step goals so later turns are possible.
No mutation of accepted assistant turns. Archive each trajectory immediately, including unfinished/censored ones.
Gate: every admitted trajectory has an honest terminal reason and all events/usage accounted for.

## 7. Phase 3 — annotate every assistant turn
Each annotation packet contains the prefix ending at that assistant response, visible task state and private verifier/reference where appropriate. It contains no later turns.
Batch at most8 short packets or4 long packets, maximum24,000 estimated input tokens per call. Do not put overlapping prefixes of the same trajectory in a single call: the longer packet would expose future context for the shorter one.
Fields: IDs/hashes, turn index, language/task, input/output and cumulative tokens, correctness, IF, memory, uncertainty/premise handling, helpfulness, safety, tone; good/minor/serious/unjudgeable; evidence; confidence; new/propagated issue; repair opportunity/success.
Serious means materially wrong/unusable task outcome, violation of a critical constraint, or consequential safety failure. Minor style imperfections do not count as serious.
Derive first serious failure and recovery state in code from prefix-local annotations. Do not force an answer for unjudgeable turns.
Root independently reviews a calibration set of24 turns (or all observed turns if fewer), stratified across labels, depth and language, plus ALL labels that define first-serious-failure or selected recovery boundaries. Use executable references for mathematical/factual decisions where possible.
If a boundary is disputed, exclude it from error-conditioned selection pending adjudication; retain it as uncertain in the raw report.
If more than20% of the reviewed calibration labels require substantive severity changes, revise the rubric and reannotate the affected class before selection. This is an operational gate, not a statistical accuracy claim. Log the actual disagreement count and reference evidence.

## 8. Phase 4 — report distribution before selecting
Produce an HTML/JSON report with:
- quality x assistant-turn1..8 heatmap, counts and denominators reaching each depth;
- first serious error distribution, no observed error, uncertainty and ending reason;
- new-failure risk among reached trajectories without earlier serious failure;
- recovery opportunities, successful repairs, delay in turns and repeated errors;
- separate task/language/context-length views; cells with fewer than5 trajectories flagged descriptive only;
- user-policy and annotation versions; planned vs actual counts; full cost/call ledger.

Each trajectory is the statistical unit for uncertainty/resampling, not each correlated turn.
No-error at natural completion is distinct from no-error observed before budget/context truncation. Report competing endings rather than inferring unseen future success.
Do not pool future enriched challenge samples into this raw distribution.
Gate: every recorded response is annotated or explicitly unjudgeable; no hidden exclusions; report frozen before sampling-prefix selection.

## 9. Phase 5 — select up to24 prefixes and branch
Full35 pilot target: maximum24 prefixes, initial6 retained-success controls plus up to18 error-related prefixes.
At reduced admitted sizes, maximum prefixes=min(24,admitted size); reserve ceil(25% of that target) for controls. These are selection-budget defaults, not final training proportions.

Eligibility:
P: immediately before the first adjudicated serious response in a trajectory.
R: first later response with an adjudicated genuine repair opportunity and the earlier mistake present. No opportunity means no R point; do not invent one.
C: successful response with no preceding serious failure, preferably from a trajectory with no observed serious failure.
At most1 P and1 R per trajectory; C uses separate trajectories where possible. Maximum2 prefixes/trajectory. Never select the same prefix twice.

Deterministic allocation:
1. Fill control quota from eligible C trajectories, spreading across early(1–2), middle(3–5), late(6–8) depths when available.
2. Apportion remaining slots between P and R in proportion to the number of eligible distinct trajectories, largest remainders. No forced50/50 split and no predetermined error depth.
3. Within P/R, apportion by observed depth bins, then spread across task/language where capacity permits; seeded sampling, not hand-picking the most dramatic examples.
4. If availability/trajectory caps make quotas infeasible, recompute from remaining eligible cells and log every redistribution. Unfilled error slots may become labelled controls; do not manufacture errors.
5. Log eligible counts, probability or deterministic inclusion rule, selected counts, seed, hashes and reasons. This is an enriched training-candidate set, not another raw frequency estimate.

For each prefix: reuse the original response plus2 fresh Apertus replies under the same decoding configuration. Up to48 extra target completions; no best-of-N changes to earlier history.
Sol ranks all3 with randomized presentation and hidden original status, evaluates acceptability, records ties and reasons. Review all exported pairs at root.
Export at most1 clear chosen/rejected pair per prefix; require an acceptable chosen answer and substantive preference. All-bad/tied sets are recorded as zero yield. No unlimited resampling; this pilot does not add a further candidate round.
Original response may be chosen, rejected or tied. Never force original=bad/new=good.
Gate: exact shared-prefix hash, valid references, no suffix leakage, no history edits, no questionable chosen response.

## 10. Phase 6 — conclusions and training handoff
Report pair yield by P/R/C, depth, task and language; severity and verified fix rate; call/token/provider cost per usable pair; number of trajectories contributing pairs.
Recommend a production P/R/C allocation based on coverage and meaningful pair yield, retaining a good-context slice. Higher pair yield is a feasibility measure, not proof of larger training benefit.
Do not use this tiny pilot to declare prevention superior to recovery.

To test actual training benefit later, propose a separately priced comparison:
- unchanged checkpoint control;
- prevention-heavy dialogue mix (60%P,20%R,20%C);
- recovery-heavy dialogue mix (20%P,60%R,20%C).
These are experimental contrasts, not recommended final proportions. Hold non-dialogue data, optimization, effective completion-token budget and source-family handling constant. Predeclare any weighting/reuse and use comparable unique trajectory support; wait for enough vetted data rather than duplicating this tiny pilot to fill quotas.
Evaluate first-error incidence, task success, recovery under a separate fixed-error-prefix suite, and the existing multilingual/safety/IF retention suite. Freeze source-disjoint evaluation before training. Re-run full raw conversations for each model; never attach the original suffix after replacing an earlier response.
Costs, sample sizes and uncertainty requirements for that training comparison remain an explicit downstream decision. No training is launched under the EUR5 inference cap.
