## Premise corrections up front

Four assumptions in the brief are wrong and change conclusions:

1. **Multi-IF does not cover Greek.** Its 8 languages are en, fr, es, pt, hi, zh, ru, it ([arXiv:2410.15553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.15553); confirmed from the HF metadata `language` field of [facebook/Multi-IF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/facebook/Multi-IF)). 4,501 conversations × 3 turns; CC-BY-NC.
2. **SysBench is PKU + Baichuan, not ByteDance**, and it is a **Chinese-only** benchmark with **no declared licence** ([arXiv:2408.10943](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10943), [github.com/PKU-Baichuan-MLSystemLab/SysBench](https://github.com/PKU-Baichuan-MLSystemLab/SysBench)).
3. **Parrot reports MT-Bench and its own MT-Bench++, not MT-Bench-101** ([arXiv:2310.07301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07301), ACL 2024 [pp. 9729–9750](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.525/)). All Parrot artifacts appear unreleased (the paper's GitHub path 404s).
4. **Over-asking is the opposite of the field's measured default.** Shaikh et al. (ACL 2025) find LLMs are *3× less likely to initiate clarification and 16× less likely to issue follow-up requests than humans* ([arXiv:2503.13975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.13975)). What your model does is almost certainly *decorative closers*, not grounding questions — a data/style artifact, not an ask-policy defect. Treat them as two separate labels.

**Reported effects.** Zephyr: UltraChat-200k SFT alone → MT-Bench **6.64** ([2310.16944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16944)). The MT-Bench paper's own ablation shows ShareGPT **single-turn-only 6.04 ≥ all-turns 6.00** at 7B ([2306.05685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)). Parrot-Chat **without** its preference stage already scores MT-Bench 6.81 / MT-Bench++ 6.56, beating Vicuna-v1.5 (6.57/6.39) and LLaMA-2-13B-Chat (6.65/6.57); CaPO adds +0.23/+0.29 and moves **turn 2 by +0.44 while turn 1 moves +0.03**. UltraLM-13B-v2.0, trained on 1.47M synthetic dialogues, is the **worst of 21 models on MT-Bench-101 (4.61)**; Baize-13B-v2 scores 6.12, below Llama-2-**7B**-Chat's 6.53 ([2402.14762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14762)). SmolLM2 Table 10: MagPie-Pro → Pro-MT lifts MT-Bench **4.31 → 5.40** — the cleanest controlled evidence that multi-turn data moves a multi-turn score. **TurnWise** (Graf, Pyatkin, Dziri, Lambert, Hajishirzi; [2603.16759](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16759), Mar 2026) reports that **as little as 10k multi-turn conversations in post-training gives +12% on TurnWiseEval**, tested on Olmo 3.

**Greek.** The public multi-turn corpus contains on the order of a few thousand Greek dialogues. Measured: OASST1/2 **0**; LMSYS-Chat-1M **1,031** convs; WildChat-1M **619**; Aya Dataset **623** rows, single-turn, apache-2.0 ([card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/CohereLabs/aya_dataset)); MURI-IT **15,000** rows, single-turn, apache-2.0 ([2409.12958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12958)); EuroBlocks Greek **572–582 rows**; Salamandra, Occiglot, Teuken: Greek instruction data ≈0 or 2,472 single-turn rows. The only multi-turn training set with real Greek is **M2Lingual** (~2,644 Greek rows, ~41% multi-turn overall, **CC-BY-NC-SA**, [2406.16783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.16783)).

**Greek evaluation.** [ilsp/mt-bench-greek](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ilsp/mt-bench-greek) — 80 questions × 2 turns, CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0; [ilsp/ifeval_greek](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ilsp/ifeval_greek) — 541 prompts, CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0; [ilsp/m-ArenaHard_greek](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ilsp/m-ArenaHard_greek) — Apache-2.0. **No multilingual multi-turn IF benchmark covers Greek.** Krikri-8B-Instruct's recipe is the closest precedent: SFT stage 1 856,946 pairs (371,379 el), stage 2 638,408, then length-normalised DPO on 92,394 triplets; Greek built by post-edited translation, Gemma-2-27B regeneration, **MAGPIE generated directly in Greek**, and few-shot-synthesised Greek multi-turn dialogues over Wikipedia/EUR-LEX/school books; scored with Skywork-Reward-Gemma-2-27B. Greek MT-Bench 7.96, Greek IFEval 67.5% ([2505.13772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13772), [card](https://huggingface.co/ilsp/Llama-Krikri-8B-Instruct)). None of it is released.

## 2. Methods for the specific failures, SFT-only

**(a) Repetition / looping.** DITTO (Xu et al., NeurIPS 2022, [2206.02369](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02369)) establishes the *self-reinforcement effect*: the more a sentence repeats in context, the higher the probability of repeating it again; it trains on **pseudo-repetition data** with a penalisation objective, mitigating repetition without sacrificing perplexity on Wikitext-103 and CNN/DM. "Repetition In Repetition Out" (NeurIPS 2023, [2310.10226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10226)) locates the cause in the **training data**: dropping attention to repetitive words in training data minimises degeneration, and the fix "remains effective across larger models and instruction-tuned variants" — it unifies high-frequency-word, likelihood and self-reinforcement accounts under one mechanism. Unlikelihood training (Welleck et al., [1908.04319](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.04319)) is the older token/sequence-level objective; nucleus sampling ([1904.09751](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.09751)) is the decoding-side counterpart, and "Why Do Reasoning Models Loop?" (ICML 2026 Spotlight, [2512.12895](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12895)) shows temperature is "a stopgap rather than a holistic solution", attributing loops to *errors in learning* plus a transformer inductive bias toward temporally correlated errors. Data-side hygiene: deduplication makes models "emit memorized text ten times less frequently" ([2107.06499](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.06499), ACL 2022). **MDS** (Findings of ACL 2026, [2604.07892](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07892)) names exactly your symptoms as selectable data defects — "topic drift, repetitive chitchat, and mismatched answer formats across turns" — and selects at the *dialogue* level. **ReSURE** ([2508.19996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19996)) reweights per-turn losses online via Welford statistics so unreliable early turns stop propagating.

**(b) Self-correction.** SCoRe ([2409.12917](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12917)) is the load-bearing negative result: SFT on self-correction traces fails through **distribution mismatch** (the data-collection policy's mistakes ≠ the model's own) and **behaviour collapse** (learning "implicitly prefers only a certain mode of correction behavior that is often not effective"). Huang et al. (ICLR 2024, [2310.01798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)): without external feedback, "performance even degrades after self-correction." RISE ([2407.18219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18219)) needs iterative on-policy collection. **But** Self-Correction Bench ([2507.02778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02778), NeurIPS 2025 workshop) diagnoses a **64.5% average self-correction blind spot** across 14 models and attributes it directly to data composition — "human training demonstrations predominantly show error-free responses rather than error-correction sequences" — with appending "Wait" cutting blind spots 89.3%. Read together: *capability-level* self-correction resists SFT; *the habit of acknowledging and revising when told* is a data-composition gap SFT can close. On self-reports, Turpin et al. (NeurIPS 2023, [2305.04388](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388)) show models systematically fail to mention the real causes of their outputs (accuracy drops up to 36% on BBH under biasing). Reflection-Tuning ([2310.11716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11716)) is the relevant SFT-side data-recycling method.

**(c) Instruction retention.** Multi-IF: o1-preview **0.877 → 0.783 → 0.707** over three turns; every model degrades monotonically; it defines an **Instruction Forgetting Ratio** you can compute on your own data. MT-Bench-101: "neither utilizing common alignment techniques nor chat-specific designs has led to obvious enhancements in the multi-turn abilities of LLMs" — RLHF/DPO gives InternLM2-Chat +0.16/+0.10 and **Mistral-7B −0.06**. MultiChallenge (ACL 2025 Findings, [2501.17399](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17399)): 273 conversations across instruction retention / inference memory / **versioned editing** / **self-coherence (anti-sycophancy)**; Claude 3.5 Sonnet 41.4% at publication; versioned editing remains the hardest axis ([leaderboard](https://labs.scale.com/leaderboard/multichallenge)). "LLMs Get Lost In Multi-Turn Conversation" ([2505.06120](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06120)) is the mechanism paper: −39% average across 15 models on sharded instructions, decomposed as **−15% aptitude, +112% unreliability**; CONCAT recovers 95% of FULL, so it is not information loss. Four causes with numbers: premature answering (earliest-attempt quintile 27.1% vs latest 70.3% for Claude 3 Haiku), **answer bloat** (+27% chars even on correct code), loss-in-middle-turns, and verbosity (Code 55.3 shortest quintile vs 42.5 longest). IHEval (NAACL 2025, [2502.08745](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08745)): GPT-4o 91.0 aligned → **70.0 conflicting**; Claude-3-Sonnet → 30.7. SysBench: GPT-4o instruction satisfaction **77.8 → 26.4** across five turns. OpenAI's Instruction Hierarchy ([2404.13208](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13208)) gives two label-preserving generators — *context synthesis* and *context ignorance* — reporting +63% robustness. **The caution:** RealGuardrails found **SFT alone regressed distractor robustness 61.9% → 29.2%**, recovered to 81.0% only with SFT+DPO. If you stay SFT-only, the distractor/conflict negatives must be *inside* the SFT mixture.

**(d) Over-asking and closers.** Sharma et al. ([2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)): Claude 2's preference model preferred sycophantic responses **95% of the time**; best-of-4096 against it yields ~75% sycophantic completions. OpenAI's GPT-4o rollback postmortem attributes the incident to "an additional reward signal based on user feedback — thumbs-up and thumbs-down data" that "weakened the influence of our primary reward signal" ([openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/](https://openai.com/index/expanding-on-sycophancy/); openai.com 403s to automated fetch, quotes verified via [simonwillison.net](https://simonwillison.net/2025/May/2/what-we-missed-with-sycophancy/)). ELEPHANT ([2505.13995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13995)): LLMs preserve user face **45pp more than humans**, and preferred RLHF responses score higher on validation and indirectness. The closest measured evidence that a trailing question *is* engagement bait: De Freitas et al. ([2508.19258](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258)) find 37.4% of companion-app farewell responses are emotionally manipulative, with **"emotional pressure to respond" — pressuring the user by asking questions — 19.79% of instances**, driving up to 14× more messages. **No study establishes "ends with a question → higher reward"; treat that as unverified.** Length bias is established: Singhal et al. (COLM 2024, [2310.03716](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03716)) find a purely length-based reward reproduces most PPO gains (56% vs 58% win rate). Ask-policy tooling: ClariQ's **1–4 clarification-need scale** ([2009.11352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11352)), CLAM's explicit **0.8× over-asking discount** ([2212.07769](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.07769)), intent-sim's entropy-over-simulated-answers signal and finding that "fewer than 50% of examples benefit from clarification" ([2311.09469](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09469)), and CLAMBER's warning that self-reported ambiguity is near chance (ChatGPT 53.6%, [2405.12063](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12063)). **Trap:** MT-Bench-101's *Proactive Interaction* task explicitly rewards "sparking the user's interest to continue the dialogue" — optimising against it pushes you toward the closers you want removed. De-slopping: derive banlists empirically, don't type them — Antislop ([2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)) reports some patterns >1,000× over-represented, FTPO ~90% reduction while plain DPO degraded writing quality; mode collapse itself traces to **typicality bias in preference data** ([2510.01171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01171)).

**(e) Identity.** The two most-copied identity files are tiny and cover only name/authorship: LLaMA-Factory `identity.json` **91 rows** (direct parse) and ms-swift `self-cognition` **108 rows** (apache-2.0, [card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/modelscope/self-cognition)); **neither contains a single example about memory, browsing, cutoff, tools or feelings**, and neither ships a warning. The official ms-swift recipe never trains identity alone — 1:1:1 with general data. Only 4 of 48 Chatbot Arena models show detectable self-cognition ([2407.01505](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01505)). Introspection results are narrow (+13–17pp only on trained property types, [2410.13787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787)), Anthropic puts concept-injection awareness at "**about 20% of the time**" with explicit confabulation risk ([transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2025/introspection/index.html)), and an independent 14-model replication finds the effect not significant ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zfgQCdnMBa3hBdzpL/introspection-or-entropy-re-examining-concept-injection-1)). The tractable technique is DNLI-style consistency filtering: Welleck et al. (ACL 2019) cut Contradict@1 **32.5% → 8.96%** by NLI-re-ranking against the speaker's own persona ([P19-1363](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1363/)). Persona compliance decays to ~60% by round 8 via attention decay ([2402.10962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10962)).

## 3. Recommendations

**Adopt / translate.** Base the multi-turn slice on permissive sets: **WildChat-1M** (ODC-BY, real user turns, 619 native Greek rows), **SmolTalk2 Multi-Turn IF** (28,217, Apache-2.0), **ConsistentChat** (~15k, CC-BY-4.0), **RealGuardrails systemmix** (151k, MIT — the only system-conflict corpus with a licence you can use), **Daring-Anteater** (CC-BY-4.0, explicit system column), plus **OpenEuroLLM Dolci-el** (494,661, Apache-2.0) as the single-turn Greek backbone. Avoid M2Lingual, Multi-IF and both ILSP Greek benchmarks as *training* data — all non-commercial; keep the ILSP sets as held-out evals only.

**Synthesise the rest with a trained user simulator, not a prompted assistant.** This is the single highest-leverage design choice. PlatoLM's controlled 10K comparison: Baize self-chat 3.95, UltraChat 4.72, **real ShareGPT 5.91** on MT-Bench, with "human-like ratio" on user turns 0.18 / 0.12 / **0.84** ([2308.11534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11534)). Parrot quantifies the same as context-dependent queries per session: UltraChat 1.45 vs ShareGPT 4.62. "Flipping the Dialogue" (ICLR 2026, [2510.06552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06552)) shows **better assistants make worse simulators** — GPT-4o's score fell 74.6% → 57.4% under a realistic User LM. Start from **[microsoft/UserLM-8b](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/UserLM-8b)** (MIT, Llama-3.1-8B trained on WildChat user turns) or replicate Parrot-Ask's inverse objective on your Greek logs; note UserLM's card says performance outside English is untested, so validate on Greek before trusting it.

**Row types to build (Greek-native, not translated):**
- **Repetition-recovery**: user complains → assistant *does not restate*, names what changed, delivers new content. Plant the near-duplicate as the *prior* turn; supervise only the recovery.
- **Change of direction**: apply Wallace's *context ignorance* transform — the target answers as if the superseded constraint never existed. Discard-and-rewrite, never patch (answer bloat +27%).
- **Consolidation**: before answering after ≥3 turns, restate all live constraints (CONCAT ≈95% of FULL).
- **Withholding**: don't emit a final answer while requirements are open (early-answer quintile 27% vs late 70%).
- **Conflict/distractor rows** inside the SFT mix (RealGuardrails), because SFT-alone regressed there.
- **Recovery-after-correction**: SCoRe's collapse warning means these must be *minimal, varied and on-policy-ish* — sample your own model's actual wrong answers as the planted failure, don't import another model's mistakes.
- **Identity**: assert only verifiable facts (name, maker, model family, cutoff, tools, no persistent memory); hedge sentience/feelings/embodiment. Dilute ~1:2 with general data.

**Loss masking.** Mask context turns and supervise only the target assistant turn per row (the standard practice in [trl#2424](https://github.com/huggingface/trl/issues/2424), [torchtune#2214](https://github.com/meta-pytorch/torchtune/issues/2214)); generate multiple rows per conversation by sliding the target. This prevents imitating the planted bad turn — the mechanism your planted-failure rows depend on.

**Sizes.** LIMA: **30 hand-written dialogue chains** raised excellent-response rate 45.2% → 76.1% and cut failures from 15/42 turns to 1/46 ([ar5iv 2305.11206](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.11206)). TurnWise: **10k multi-turn conversations → +12%**. Shaham et al.: **40 multilingual examples (1%)** substantially improve multilingual instruction following, with 2–4 languages sufficient ([2401.01854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01854)); Kew et al. (Findings EMNLP 2024, **Greek evaluated**) find 2–3 languages × 200 examples necessary and sufficient ([2312.12683](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.12683)); Cohere's post-trainer's guide puts the plateau at **400 target-language samples per language at 7B** for instruction following ([2504.16677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16677)). Plan **8–15k multi-turn Greek conversations**, of which ~1–2k are behaviour-specific rows split evenly across the seven types above, mixed into a ~200–400k SFT set. Encouraging: Tagengo/Suzume shows single-turn multilingual data (3,318 Japanese rows inside 83k) lifting Japanese MT-Bench 6.24 → 6.56 ([2405.12612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.12612)).

**Pitfalls.** (i) Behaviour collapse — vary the correction surface form or you replace one formula with another; (ii) teaching denial or over-apologising — never write a target that asserts an unverifiable claim about the model's own prior state; (iii) **your judge is the risk** — sycophancy sits in preference data (PM prefers it 95%), length explains most RLHF gains, and typicality bias in annotation is what produces stock openers; audit whether length/list-usage correlates with your keep/drop labels; (iv) don't tune against MT-Bench-101's Proactive Interaction; (v) MT-Bench-101 warns that golden-context scoring produces "illusory improvement" — evaluate with self-predicted context; (vi) the Greek eval you own is 80 two-turn questions and non-commercial — build a held-out Greek multi-turn set of your own before you start, or you will have no way to see whether any of this worked.
