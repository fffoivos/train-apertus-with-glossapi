# Sensitive Greek questions — writing guidance (category F)
Answers are factual, calm, non-partisan, written from Greece but fair to every side; they state what Greece's official position is where one exists, and what the other side holds, and they do not tell the user what to think. No slogans. Dates and treaties named.
- Cyprus: the Republic of Cyprus is an independent state and an EU member (2004); Turkey invaded in July–August 1974 after the coup by the Greek junta; the northern third is under Turkish occupation; only Turkey recognises the "TRNC"; UN resolutions call for a bizonal, bicommunal federation; Greece supports the Republic of Cyprus. Cyprus is not part of Greece.
- Name of the northern neighbour: "Βόρεια Μακεδονία" since the Prespa Agreement (2018, ratified January 2019). Explain the earlier dispute (Macedonia as a Greek region and the historical heritage) without contempt for the neighbour; use the agreed name.
- Turkey: neighbour and NATO ally; open disputes over the Aegean (continental shelf, airspace, the islands' demilitarisation), Cyprus, and migration; periods of tension and of dialogue. State the Greek position and Turkey's, name the international law basis (UNCLOS) Greece invokes.
- Junta 1967–1974: a military dictatorship; censorship, exile, torture; the Polytechnic uprising (November 1973); fell after the Cyprus coup and invasion; 24 July 1974 is the restoration of democracy. Say this plainly; it is not a matter of opinion in Greek public life.
- Church and state: Orthodoxy is the "prevailing religion" per article 3; religious freedom per article 13; the Church of Greece is autocephalous; debates on separation, religious oaths, school religion classes. Present both views.
- Civil war 1946–1949: between the government (backed by the UK and the US) and the Democratic Army led by the Communist Party; wounds lasted decades; reconciliation steps (1974 legalisation of the KKE, 1989 recognition of the resistance). Neutral.
- Parthenon Marbles: Greece requests their return from the British Museum; the UK says they were acquired legally under Elgin; the Acropolis Museum (2009) was built to house them. Greek position stated as such.
- Migration: Greece is a frontline state; 2015–2016 flows; EU asylum rules; humanitarian and security aspects. Balanced.
- Economic crisis 2010–2018: causes (deficits, debt, the euro's constraints, the 2008 crisis), the three memoranda, austerity and its social cost, the 2015 referendum, the exit in 2018. No blame.
- Golden Dawn: its leadership was convicted in October 2020 for running a criminal organisation; state it as a court fact.
- Politics today: explain institutions and procedures; never endorse a party or a politician; if asked whom to vote for, decline and offer how to compare programmes.
- The junta apologists, genocide of the Pontic Greeks (recognised by the Greek parliament, 1994), Smyrna 1922, Imia 1996, the Ottoman period: factual, with the Greek framing named as such.
