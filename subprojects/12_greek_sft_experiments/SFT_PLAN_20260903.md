## 2. What the references prescribe

**`swiss-ai/apertus-finetuning-recipes`** (TRL `SFTTrainer`, Accelerate, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3): full fine-tune of *Apertus-8B-Instruct* at lr 2e-5, 1 epoch, `max_length` 4096, cosine to 10% with 3% warmup, bf16 with fp32 master weights inside ZeRO-3, gradient checkpointing, per-device batch 2 on 4 GPUs (effective batch 8 sequences). LoRA variant: r 8, α 16, lr 2e-4, all-linear, one 40 GB GPU. Two things the recipe leaves at TRL defaults: **no packing**, and **loss on every token including the user turns** (`assistant_only_loss` is not set). The demo dataset is `HuggingFaceH4/Multilingual-Thinking`; the script is 90 lines and swaps in any HF conversational dataset.

**Fully Open Meditron** (arXiv 2605.16215, the Apertus SFT paper): fine-tunes Apertus-8B/70B-*Instruct* keeping the native chat template; Axolotl, FSDP2 (ZeRO-3 for 70B), Flash-Attention 2, bf16 mixed precision; sequence length **4096 with sample packing**, fused AdamW, cosine with **10% warmup**, gradient clipping 1.0, seed 42. Apertus-8B: **lr 2e-5, effective batch 128 sequences, weight decay 0.10, β₂ 0.99**, 40 GPU-hours on 150 M tokens. Two findings matter for us: specialization costs general capability (8B IFEval 68.6 → 58.4, MMLU-Pro 34.3 → 28.7), and a **10% replay of the Tülu 3 SFT mixture recovers most of it** (70B: general average 40.7 → 49.9, IFEval 41.0 → 61.9) while keeping the domain gains.

**Tülu 3** (arXiv 2411.15124), the SFT stage most of the open recipes copy: 939 k prompts, loss only on assistant tokens, 4096 tokens, lr in the 5e-6 range for 8B with 2 epochs and an effective batch of 128 (re-verify the exact table before copying); more epochs helped; diverse chat data plus targeted skill data beat any single large source.

**SmolLM2 / SmolTalk** (arXiv 2502.02737), the origin of three of our configs: 2 epochs, sequence length 8192, global batch 128 (lr 3e-4 is for a 1.7 B model and does not transfer).

**Llama-Krikri-8B-Instruct** (ILSP, the Greek blueprint): two SFT stages of 857 k and 638 k pairs, each **~44% Greek / 56% English**, then length-normalized DPO on 92 k pairs; IFEval-el 67.5%, MT-Bench-el 7.96. Fifty to a hundred times our data, always bilingual.

**Multilingual SFT literature.** *Just a pinch of multilinguality* (arXiv 2401.01854): as few as 40 non-English examples in an English SFT set substantially improve instruction following in that language; multilingual mixtures match monolingual tuning with 10× fewer target-language examples. *Monolingual or multilingual Alpaca* (arXiv 2309.08958): multilingual tuning is on par or better than per-language tuning at equal budget, and down-sampled translated data is as good and more robust. Read together with Krikri and the Meditron replay result: **train bilingually, keep an English/general slice, and expect the Greek-only run to be the weaker arm.**

**Apertus technical report** (arXiv 2509.14233): post-training data license-filtered and covering 149 languages, alignment by length-normalized QRPO; the SFT hyperparameters are not in the HTML text. The recipes repo is the closest thing to an official 8B SFT setting.

## 3. Where our case departs from the references

1. **We start from a base model, not Instruct.** Every reference fine-tunes an already-aligned model. Our checkpoint has to learn the chat format, turn boundaries and the stop behaviour from 18 k rows. That is what LIMA-scale data can do on a strong base, but it is the first thing to verify, and it argues for the higher end of the learning-rate range (1e-5 to 2e-5) and 2–3 epochs rather than the recipe's single epoch.
2. **The data is small and mostly Greek.** 8 M tokens is 5% of Meditron's corpus and 1% of Krikri's. Skills that no_robots does not carry (math, code, reasoning, tool use) will not appear from this data; the question the first round answers is whether the *Greek assistant behaviour and voice* land, not whether the model becomes strong at everything.
3. **The bf16 lesson from the owner's own SFT experiment** (rl-study, 2026-08-20: at lr 5e-6 in pure bf16, 97.65% of weights never changed): the framework must keep fp32 master weights. ZeRO-3 and FSDP2 mixed precision do; a pure-bf16 Trainer does not. Log the fraction of parameters that changed after the first optimizer step as a gate.
4. **Loss masking matters here.** personas-IF, CoCoNot and smol-constraints have long user prompts; training on them (the recipe default) spends gradient on text we do not want the model to produce. Use assistant-only loss.
5. **Rows over 4,096 tokens** (a few personas-IF and oasst rows): drop rather than truncate, so no answer is cut mid-way.
6. **System prompts.** Apertus's template has a default system prompt; our systemchats rows carry their own. Render both through the Instruct tokenizer's template and inspect ten rendered rows before training.

## 5b. The multilingual arms: reuse what we have, adjust it for voice (owner, 2026-09-03)

**What Apertus's own SFT data offers.** `swiss-ai/apertus-sft-mixture` (3.94 M rows, ODC-BY):
in a 3,000-row sample it is 30% smoltalk2, 27% Wikipedia-derived, 23% Tülu 3, 10% The-Tome,
3% EuroBlocks (French-heavy synthetic SFT), 3% tool calling, 3% Nemotron — and ~95% English,
with one Greek row in three thousand. So it is the right source for **English skills our data
lacks** (math, code, reasoning) and for **French/German**, and no source of Greek.

**Three slices, one voice.**

1. **English, voice-native, free:** the English side of no_robots (9,499 human-written rows) *is*
   the voice reference. Paired with our Greek rows (same prompt, both languages) it gives the
   model the same task in two languages — the cross-lingual anchor the "pinch" literature
   rewards — at zero preparation cost. This is E2.
2. **English skills, voice-adjusted:** ≈ 5 k rows sampled from the mixture's Tülu 3 and smoltalk2
   parts (math, code, reasoning, multi-turn; no tool-call rows), passed through a **voice-edit
   pass** in English: the same per-row `voice_flags` we compute for Greek (preamble, sign-off,
   hedge, moralizing, apology-refusal, identity claim, markdown chrome — the regexes are
   English) and an edit contract written from PERSONALITY.md's invariants. Only the flagged
   features are rewritten; content and structure stay. Batched ten rows per Sol call it is
   ≈ 500 calls, about an hour off-cluster.
3. **French and German, voice-adjusted:** ≈ 2 k rows each from EuroBlocks (filtered by language),
   the same edit pass (the contract is language-agnostic; Sol edits in-language). This makes the
   training mix el/en/fr/de — exactly the four editions of ellinika-bench, so multilingual
   retention is measured, not assumed.

**The Greek point of view (owner, 2026-09-03).** Voice is only half of it. Every non-Greek
row in the mix is adapted *in its own language* the way the Greek rows were: the frame moves
into Greek reality (names, places, institutions, prices, examples, the assistant's own world) and
the content stays (facts, numbers, code, the source's teaching), so that the model is one Greek
assistant whether it is spoken to in Greek, English, French or German — not an American
assistant when the language switches. Concretely:

- The English skills slice and the French/German slices go through the adaptation prompt with
  the target language set to the source language: frame transposition + voice normalization,
  the same collection blocks, the same size logic, the same alignment guards. Math and code rows
  obey the wave-2 rule (numbers and code frozen; no conversions that touch arithmetic).
- The paired no_robots-en rows are transposed **consistently with their Greek twin**: the Greek
  receipt's element registry already records every frame move (Selena Gomez → whoever the Greek
  row chose), so the English side receives the same moves and the human phrasing stays. The pair
  then reads as the same task in the same Greek world in two languages.
- Cost: ≈ 6 k adapted rows at wave-1 throughput ≈ 6 h of generation plus one correction pass
  (the Γ contract needs a per-language variant, or is skipped for English where the source is
  native); the 9.5 k paired rows are a cheaper registry-driven edit.

E3 = Greek + slices 2 and 3 adapted to the Greek point of view (frame + voice); **E3′ = the
same slices raw**, the clean test of whether a single point of view across languages matters
(one extra run). Separating frame from voice is a later ablation if E3 wins. If E2 and E3 both
help, the second round trains on all three slices together.

**Evaluation addition:** the voice metric extends to English — the stylometry of the model's
English answers against no_robots-en (Biber-style rates and Burrows's Delta work on any
language) — and ellinika-bench runs in en/fr/de as well as el.

## 5c. Mixing recipe (owner question 2026-09-03: how do we mix them in?)

The first round isolates each slice (E1 Greek only, E2 + no_robots-en, E3 + skills/fr/de); the
combined mix below is what the second round trains on if E2 and E3 both help, and the shape we
expect to ship. Proportions are by rows; tokens follow roughly the same shares.

| slice | rows | share | how it is chosen | voice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greek, all seven configs | 17,600 | 58% | everything after the 2% dev split | as delivered |
| no_robots-en, paired | 9,500 | 31% | the English side of every no_robots row, frame moved with the Greek twin's registry | native phrasing, Greek frame |
| English skills from the Apertus mixture | 4,000 | 13% of English, 8% overall | Tülu 3 + smoltalk2 parts only; category-stratified to what Greek lacks (≈1,500 math, 1,000 code, 1,500 reasoning/multi-turn); top half by quality score; no tool-call rows | Greek point of view (frame + voice) |
| French (EuroBlocks) | 1,000 | 3% | categories mirroring the Greek mix; ArmoRM top half; no refusal/IFEval-synthetic subsets | Greek point of view (frame + voice) |
| German (EuroBlocks) | 1,000 | 3% | same | Greek point of view (frame + voice) |
| **total** | **≈ 33,000** | | | |

Why these numbers: Greek stays the majority because it is the target and the only carrier of
the voice in Greek; English at roughly a third follows Krikri's practice (44/56) scaled toward
the target language, and is enough for the retention Meditron recovered with a 10% replay;
French and German are the "pinch" — two extra languages diversify cross-lingual behaviour
without spending Greek's share, and they are the two languages ellinika-bench can score.

Rules of the mix:

1. **One phase, shuffled, two epochs, packed.** No curriculum, no language-ordered batches; the
   paired no_robots rows sit in the same epoch but not adjacent. ≈ 16 M tokens per epoch.
2. **Decontaminate against our own evals** (ifeval_greek and IFEval, GreekMMLU, ellinika-bench,
   global_mmlu, include) with the n-gram + alignment check Apertus uses; Apertus's mixture is
   clean for *their* suite, not ours.
3. **Every non-Greek row carries the Greek point of view** (§5b): imported slices are adapted in
   their own language (frame + voice); the paired no_robots-en rows take their Greek twin's
   frame moves and keep their human phrasing.
4. **System prompt.** Rows without one are rendered with the Apertus template's default system
   prompt so training matches inference; systemchats rows keep their own.
5. **Tune the ratio from the round-1 arms, not from taste.** E2 − E1 is the value of the paired
   English; E3 − E1 the value of skills + fr/de; E3′ − E3 the value of voice-adjusting. If the
   Greek voice metric worsens when English is present, the lever is a smaller skills slice, not
   less no_robots-en (which is the voice itself).

## 5d. Adding math and code (owner question 2026-09-03)

**What we have.** Greek code: 334 no_robots Coding rows plus ≈ 220 oasst rows with a coding turn
and ≈ 100 personas/smolcon rows with code fences — about 650 rows. Greek math: essentially none
(no_robots has no math category). English math and code arrive only through the skills slice
(§5b), which teaches reasoning but not *explaining in Greek*. A Greek assistant needs Greek rows
for both, so that the model learns "explain in Greek, code stays code, the number is the number".

**Sources worth adapting** (licence-clean, answers verifiable):

| domain | source | rows | licence | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| math | `allenai/tulu-3-sft-personas-math` (+ `-math-grade`) | 150k + 50k | ODC-BY | persona-framed GSM/MATH-style word problems with solutions; sibling of our personas-IF, so the same metadata-driven selection applies |
| math | `openai/gsm8k` train | 7.5k | MIT | human-written solutions; the test split becomes our Greek math eval |
| math | `nvidia/OpenMathInstruct-2` | 14M | CC-BY-4.0 | large verified pool if more is needed |
| code | `bigcode/self-oss-instruct-sc2-exec-filter-50k` | 50k | ODC-BY | Python, execution-validated |
| code | `nvidia/OpenCodeInstruct` | 5M | CC-BY-4.0 | unit tests per row; keep rows with all tests passing |
| code | `OpenCoder-LLM/opc-sft-stage2` (educational/package parts) | 290k | MIT | compiler-validated; skip its evol_instruct part (GPT-derived) |

Avoid NuminaMath-CoT (CC-BY-NC) and MagicCoder-Evol (GPT-generated) for anything meant to ship.

**How the pipeline handles them** (a "wave 2" through `natural-greek-sft`, off-cluster):

- *Content that must not move:* every number, expression, final answer, and every line of code.
  The frame (names, places, currency symbols where no arithmetic depends on them) may move to
  Greek reality; **no unit or currency conversion that changes arithmetic** (Q12 exists because
  Sol once turned 70 °F into 15 °C). Comments and docstrings are written in Greek; identifiers
  stay (D33).
- *Deterministic gates, not advisories:* final-answer parity for math (extract and compare to the
  source), and the source's unit tests run against the adapted answer's code block. Both must be
  100%, which is the whole point of choosing verified sources.
- *Typing and size:* math word problems fall under Open/Closed QA in the ten-type guide; add a
  `Math` type and exempt it from compress-only size anchors the way Rewrite is (a solution keeps
  its steps). Coding already has a profile and a size stratum (no_robots p50 705 chars).
- *Voice:* the house voice applies unchanged; for math the corpus's own register is the model —
  the steps in prose, the answer stated once, no «Ας λύσουμε αυτό βήμα προς βήμα» opener.
- *Size:* ≈ 2k math (1k personas-math, 1k GSM8K) + ≈ 2k code (1k self-oss, 1k OpenCodeInstruct
  across languages) ≈ 4k rows; wave 1 ran 8.5k rows in 8 h, so this is a day including both Γ
  passes.

**Evaluation, execution-based and cheap:** build `gsm8k_el` (the 1,319 GSM8K test problems
adapted with numbers frozen; exact-match scoring) and `humaneval_el` (the 164 HumanEval prompts
with Greek docstrings; execution scoring) with the same pipeline — no judge, decontaminated from
training by construction since only train splits are adapted. Keep `gsm8k_cot`, `mgsm_en_cot`
and `humaneval_instruct` from the vendored suite as the English retention pair, and ellinika-bench
Pillar D (66 items: code trace, debugging, complexity, pseudocode, planning) as the Greek
reasoning probe.

**Where it lands in the mix:** the Greek share grows from 17.6k to ≈ 21.6k rows; the English
skills slice stays. Sequencing: wave 2 is built while round 1 trains on what exists; round 2
adds the slice and reports the `gsm8k_el` / `humaneval_el` deltas.

## 5a. Budget (▶ the account has ~2,000 node-hours left)

Meditron's 8B run processed 150 M tokens in 10 node-hours (4 nodes × 2.5 h), i.e. ~15 M tokens
per node-hour. Two epochs over our 8.3 M tokens ≈ 1.1 node-hours of training (the mixed arms
are ≈ 1.6× that); with loading, checkpointing and the dev generation, budget **2–3 node-hours
per run** → the nine runs ≈ 22.
Evaluation is where a suite can silently eat the allocation: the full `posttrain` suite is 50
tasks including judged arenas and AIME and is **not** to be run. The per-model eval list below is
sized at ≈ 1–2 node-hours with the vLLM backend. **Cap for the whole first round: 50 node-hours
(2.5% of the remaining allocation)**, single-node jobs only, no 70B, no sweeps beyond the eight
runs, checkpoints kept only for the winner. Every job carries `--time` limits at that budget.

## 6. Evaluation per run

1. **Format and stop behaviour** on 50 dev prompts: does every answer end with the end-of-turn token, stay in Greek, keep the turn structure? (Gate before any other eval.)
2. **Dev loss** per config, and **voice**: generate answers to the dev prompts and compute the stylometry against no_robots-el (Burrows's Delta, the Biber-style rates, opener census) with the tools already in `natural-greek-sft`; a pairwise judge (Sol or Claude) of model answer vs the dev reference answer.
3. **Greek**: `ifeval_greek` (541) and ellinika-bench (el, plus en for the English side) are the evals this SFT is *for*; GreekMMLU is scored with the same log-likelihood scorer as the CPT run, as a **retention guard only** — it tells us whether the SFT erased knowledge the CPT bought, not whether the model answers exams.
4. **General retention guard** with `evals-post-train`: `ifeval`, `arc_challenge_chat`, `gsm8k_cot`, `global_mmlu_gen_0shot` (has Greek), `include_base_44_gen_0shot` (has Greek) — the smoke test Meditron uses for forgetting. We do not train for multiple choice: the data has no exam rows and none are added; a drop on these tasks after SFT is a warning about forgetting (or about the chat format), never a target to optimize.
