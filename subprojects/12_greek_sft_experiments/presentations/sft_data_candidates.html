Round Two Data Candidates

The open post-training sets worth considering for stage one, what is inside them, and an experiment that tells us which of them actually move Greek instruction following before we commit a large run.

1. The candidatesFourteen open sets, sizes and licences verified on Hugging Face today. Five were released in 2026.
2. Their contentsDomains, sources, and real rows from each family, including the constraint sets that match our weakest benchmark rows.
3. The testNine arms on the current recipe, about CHF 75, with the noise floor already measured.

Everything here was checked against the Hugging Face API, the dataset cards and sample rows on 2026-09-04. Sizes are row counts from the dataset metadata; where the server and the card disagree, the card number is used and the disagreement noted.

Part 3 · the test

One recipe, one base, eight dose-matched arms

Every arm is our 20k Greek rows plus 30,000 rows of one candidate, trained with the round-one recipe on the averaged CPT base, learning rate 1e-5, two epochs, one seed. The control is the pick we already have. The noise floor is measured: about 1 point on Greek IFEval prompt-level between seeds, 0.03 on MGSM, 0.05 on the voice score.

A. ifeval-like, filteredChecker-verified English constraint rows. The purest test of transfer.
B. Dolci Precise IFPersona-grounded constraints, the family Tulu and OLMo use.
C. Nemotron IF v3Human prompts, GLM-5 answers, reasoning stripped. The 2026 version of the same idea.
D. Nemotron structured outputsSchema tasks only. Tests the JSON and format types.
E. Generic chat controlNemotron chat v3 or Magpie Ultra. Does any English SFT lift Greek IFEval, or only constraint data?
F. Math controlOpenMathInstruct-2 sample. Expect MGSM up and IFEval flat. If IFEval moves too, the gain is general.
G. DoseThe best of A to D at 100k rows. Does it keep rising?
H. UnionA + B + C + D at 30k total. Do they add up or overlap?
I. DPO on the pickNo new SFT data. Preference pairs only: the 18k precise-IF pairs from Luciole, or Greek constraint pairs scored by our checkers. Measures what preference optimisation alone is worth here.

Part 3 · the test

What we measure, and what counts as an improvement

Measured on every arm

  • Greek IFEval, strict average, and the pass rate per instruction type. The browser page shows where the points came from.
  • English IFEval, the original, so transfer and Greek-specific gains can be told apart. Not run in round one; about 12 minutes per arm.
  • Greek MGSM, the format gate, the voice score against no_robots-el, and the blind tone reading by Claude on 40 prompts.

On the winner only

  • The native-Greek suite, GreekMMLU, the 40 interviews, and a second seed.

An arm improves instruction following if

  • Greek IFEval rises by at least 3 points over the control, three times the seed floor.
  • The gain shows on the target types: no commas, two responses, placeholders, paragraphs.
  • The voice score does not worsen by more than 0.05, and the tone reading shows no rise in mannerisms or commands.
  • Greek MGSM stays within 0.03.

Before any arm trains

  • Decontamination: every candidate's prompts against the English originals of Greek IFEval and against GSM8K test, exact and near-duplicate. The Greek benchmarks are translations, so English leakage counts.
  • The 4,096-token window filter, and reasoning fields stripped from Nemotron rows.

Order: A, B, C, E and I first, since they answer the main questions, constraint data versus generic English, and SFT data versus preference optimisation, in one workbench day. F and D next. G and H only if something in the first four moved.

What the answer buys: the composition of stage one. If A or C moves Greek IFEval by 5 points and E does not, stage one is built around constraint data and the rest of the mixture is there for breadth, not for this benchmark. If E moves it as much as A, volume is what matters and the M tier is the right call. If nothing moves it beyond the floor, instruction following in Greek has to come from Greek constraint data and stage three, and the broad suite is justified only by the other axes.

Budget note. CHF 52 of the round-one cap of 90 is spent and GreekMMLU adds about 9. The first four arms fit under the cap. The full test needs the cap raised.
