The idea

Instruction following you can verify with code

Most chat evaluations ask a stronger model to grade the answer. IFEval sidesteps that. Every prompt bundles a task with formal constraints, and each constraint has a deterministic checker. The task itself is never graded: a bad essay in the right shape passes, a brilliant essay in the wrong shape fails.

ILSP translated all 541 prompts by hand and localised the values the checkers look for: keywords, forbidden words, the postscript marker «Υ.Γ.», section markers such as ΕΝΟΤΗΤΑ and ΠΑΡΑΓΡΑΦΟΣ. Numeric thresholds and the target languages stayed as in the English original.

Scoring

Four numbers, and which one people quote

Prompt level

A prompt counts as passed only if every instruction in it passes. The Raymond III prompt above fails for both models.

Instruction level

Each of the 834 instructions is counted on its own. The same prompt gives both models 2 of 3.

Strict

The checkers run on the answer as written.

Loose

The checkers also try the answer with markdown asterisks removed and with its first or last line dropped, and take the best. This forgives "Here is your answer:" and bold markers.

The headline number, the one on the Krikri model card and in our tables, is the strict average: the mean of prompt-level strict and instruction-level strict.

Generation settings in ILSP's protocol and ours: the model's chat template, zero-shot, greedy decoding, up to 1,280 new tokens. The loose and strict scores are close for our models (47.9% vs 49.5% at prompt level), so presentation is not what costs us points.

Greek-aware checking

Where a naive port would break

  • Capitals with tonos. «Ά» counts as upper case; the all-caps and all-lower checks use Unicode-aware tests on Greek letters only.
  • Accents. Keyword and letter counts strip accents and fold case, so «αθλητικά» matches «ΑΘΛΗΤΙΚΑ» and a target letter «ο» also counts «ό».
  • Sentences. The Greek question mark is a semicolon «;». The sentence splitter treats it as an end of sentence.
  • Markers. The postscript is «Υ.Γ.» and counts only at the start of a line. Section markers are the Greek words the prompt gives: ΕΝΟΤΗΤΑ, Ενότητα, Ημέρα, Κοινό, ΠΑΡΑΓΡΑΦΟΣ.
  • Words. Whitespace tokens with punctuation trimmed from the edges; a token that is only punctuation is not a word.

Reading the table

Counting is our gap, not formatting

Where we lose most to Krikri

  • No commas, the most common instruction: 0.55 vs 0.97. Ours cannot hold a rule across a long text.
  • Two responses: 0.17 vs 0.83. Ours writes one.
  • Placeholders, paragraphs, letter counts: anything that requires counting while writing.

Where we hold or lead

  • JSON only: 0.88 vs 0.71. Ours writes plain valid JSON; Krikri decorates it.
  • Repeat the request first: 0.59 vs 0.29. Krikri puts a title before the repeat and fails the "nothing before" rule.
  • Title, fixed replies, all caps: level or ahead.
A hole in our harness. "Respond only in language X" scores 0 for every model, Gemma included, and Gemma plainly answers in Kannada when asked (86% of its characters are Kannada script). The checker needs a language-detection library that is missing on the cluster; without it only Greek can be recognised. All 31 such instructions fail automatically, 17 of them as single-instruction prompts. If every one passed, the strict average would rise by up to 3.5 points for each model (the pick 53.3 → 56.7, Krikri 66.8 → 70.3), so rankings hold but every absolute number here is a floor. The saved answers can be rescored on a laptop once the library is installed. No GPU time is needed.

Limits

What it measures, and what it cannot

It measures

  • Whether a model reads a constraint and keeps it for the whole answer.
  • Whether the model stops cleanly. An answer that runs on fails length and end-phrase checks.
  • Robustness of Greek text handling: accents, capitals, the «;» question mark.

It cannot see

  • Whether the content is true, useful, or well written. Nonsense in all caps passes.
  • Tone, warmth, a Greek point of view. Our blind reading covers those.
  • Whether the model was trained on IFEval-shaped data. Krikri was, and that is legitimate, but it means the score reflects the training mix as much as the base.

It is also a small benchmark. With 541 prompts the standard error on prompt-level accuracy is about 2 points, and our seed-to-seed spread was 1 point. Differences under 2 points between models are noise.

Takeaways

What would move our score

  • Constraint-shaped training data. Our Greek data has none; the imported slices with formal constraints appear only in the adapted-imports run, which is 1.6 points above the pick. A few thousand Greek rows with counted words, banned commas and paired responses would target the exact rows we fail.
  • Fix the language check and rescore the saved answers. Up to 3.5 points for everyone, no GPU, no training.
  • Epoch 3 over epoch 2. The extra epoch adds 3 points on this benchmark and reads better in the blind evaluation. The dev loss said stop at 2; the behaviour says 3.
  • Do not chase it alone. Greek CPT plus our SFT matches Apertus-Instruct here and trails it on Greek math. Instruction following is one axis; the reading, the native suite and the interviews are the others.
