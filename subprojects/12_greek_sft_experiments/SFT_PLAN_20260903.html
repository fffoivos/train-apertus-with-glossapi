How to start SFT on the natural-Greek data — recipes examined, first experiments proposed

Date: 2026-09-03. Status: proposal, nothing run. Owner decisions needed: marked ▶.

1. What we have

Base modelfffoivos/apertus-8b-greek-cpt, default revision 18-avg-uniform5-tokens30B-50B (the five-checkpoint peak-window average from subproject 09: GreekMMLU 56.78%, best retention). It is a base model: Apertus-8B-2509 continued on 76.7 B Greek tokens; it has never seen a chat template.
Datafffoivos/natural-greek-sft-no-robots, 7 configs, 17,968 rows ≈ 8.3 M tokens (5.7 M on the assistant side). 10% of rows carry a system prompt (all of systemchats, 8% of no_robots), 27% are multi-turn. Median row ≈ 1,050 characters; p99 ≈ 6,000; a handful exceed 4,096 tokens and must be truncated or dropped. Every row also carries its English original (en), so a bilingual mix costs nothing to build.
ComputeClariden (GH200, 4 GPUs per node), access refreshed. The Meditron paper's 8B run processed ~150 M tokens in 2.5 h on 16 GPUs; at that rate two epochs over our 8.3 M tokens are well under one node-hour. A run is cheap; a grid of ten is an afternoon.
Greek evals in handGreekMMLU (frozen decontaminated set from 09, retention of CPT knowledge); ilsp/ifeval_greek (541 prompts, IFEval translated, CC-BY-NC-SA); our own ellinika-bench (449 items, four pillars, el/en/fr/de, Claude as judge, already has a Clariden runner); the held-out slice of our own data with the stylometric distance to no_robots-el as a voice metric.
General evalsswiss-ai/evals-post-train (vendored): the posttrain suite runs on Clariden through lm-eval with a vLLM backend; global_mmlu and include contain Greek; multi-if does not.

2. What the references prescribe

swiss-ai/apertus-finetuning-recipes (TRL SFTTrainer, Accelerate, DeepSpeed ZeRO-3): full fine-tune of Apertus-8B-Instruct at lr 2e-5, 1 epoch, max_length 4096, cosine to 10% with 3% warmup, bf16 with fp32 master weights inside ZeRO-3, gradient checkpointing, per-device batch 2 on 4 GPUs (effective batch 8 sequences). LoRA variant: r 8, α 16, lr 2e-4, all-linear, one 40 GB GPU. Two things the recipe leaves at TRL defaults: no packing, and loss on every token including the user turns (assistant_only_loss is not set). The demo dataset is HuggingFaceH4/Multilingual-Thinking; the script is 90 lines and swaps in any HF conversational dataset.

Fully Open Meditron (arXiv 2605.16215, the Apertus SFT paper): fine-tunes Apertus-8B/70B-Instruct keeping the native chat template; Axolotl, FSDP2 (ZeRO-3 for 70B), Flash-Attention 2, bf16 mixed precision; sequence length 4096 with sample packing, fused AdamW, cosine with 10% warmup, gradient clipping 1.0, seed 42. Apertus-8B: lr 2e-5, effective batch 128 sequences, weight decay 0.10, β₂ 0.99, 40 GPU-hours on 150 M tokens. Two findings matter for us: specialization costs general capability (8B IFEval 68.6 → 58.4, MMLU-Pro 34.3 → 28.7), and a 10% replay of the Tülu 3 SFT mixture recovers most of it (70B: general average 40.7 → 49.9, IFEval 41.0 → 61.9) while keeping the domain gains.

Tülu 3 (arXiv 2411.15124), the SFT stage most of the open recipes copy: 939 k prompts, loss only on assistant tokens, 4096 tokens, lr in the 5e-6 range for 8B with 2 epochs and an effective batch of 128 (re-verify the exact table before copying); more epochs helped; diverse chat data plus targeted skill data beat any single large source.

SmolLM2 / SmolTalk (arXiv 2502.02737), the origin of three of our configs: 2 epochs, sequence length 8192, global batch 128 (lr 3e-4 is for a 1.7 B model and does not transfer).

Llama-Krikri-8B-Instruct (ILSP, the Greek blueprint): two SFT stages of 857 k and 638 k pairs, each ~44% Greek / 56% English, then length-normalized DPO on 92 k pairs; IFEval-el 67.5%, MT-Bench-el 7.96. Fifty to a hundred times our data, always bilingual.

Multilingual SFT literature. Just a pinch of multilinguality (arXiv 2401.01854): as few as 40 non-English examples in an English SFT set substantially improve instruction following in that language; multilingual mixtures match monolingual tuning with 10× fewer target-language examples. Monolingual or multilingual Alpaca (arXiv 2309.08958): multilingual tuning is on par or better than per-language tuning at equal budget, and down-sampled translated data is as good and more robust. Read together with Krikri and the Meditron replay result: train bilingually, keep an English/general slice, and expect the Greek-only run to be the weaker arm.

Apertus technical report (arXiv 2509.14233): post-training data license-filtered and covering 149 languages, alignment by length-normalized QRPO; the SFT hyperparameters are not in the HTML text. The recipes repo is the closest thing to an official 8B SFT setting.

3. Where our case departs from the references

  1. We start from a base model, not Instruct. Every reference fine-tunes an already-aligned model. Our checkpoint has to learn the chat format, turn boundaries and the stop behaviour from 18 k rows. That is what LIMA-scale data can do on a strong base, but it is the first thing to verify, and it argues for the higher end of the learning-rate range (1e-5 to 2e-5) and 2–3 epochs rather than the recipe's single epoch.
  2. The data is small and mostly Greek. 8 M tokens is 5% of Meditron's corpus and 1% of Krikri's. Skills that no_robots does not carry (math, code, reasoning, tool use) will not appear from this data; the question the first round answers is whether the Greek assistant behaviour and voice land, not whether the model becomes strong at everything.
  3. The bf16 lesson from the owner's own SFT experiment (rl-study, 2026-08-20: at lr 5e-6 in pure bf16, 97.65% of weights never changed): the framework must keep fp32 master weights. ZeRO-3 and FSDP2 mixed precision do; a pure-bf16 Trainer does not. Log the fraction of parameters that changed after the first optimizer step as a gate.
  4. Loss masking matters here. personas-IF, CoCoNot and smol-constraints have long user prompts; training on them (the recipe default) spends gradient on text we do not want the model to produce. Use assistant-only loss.
  5. Rows over 4,096 tokens (a few personas-IF and oasst rows): drop rather than truncate, so no answer is cut mid-way.
  6. System prompts. Apertus's template has a default system prompt; our systemchats rows carry their own. Render both through the Instruct tokenizer's template and inspect ten rendered rows before training.

5. Proposed first round (one afternoon of node-hours)

Common settings unless varied: full fine-tune, 4096 with packing, assistant-only loss, AdamW (wd 0.1, β₂ 0.99), cosine to 10% with 10% warmup, effective batch 64 sequences (≈ 270 steps per epoch), 2 epochs, bf16 mixed precision with fp32 master weights, seed 42, gradient clipping 1.0. Train/dev split: 2% of every config held out (≈ 360 rows) with the same subcategory strata.

5a. Budget (▶ the account has ~2,000 node-hours left)

Meditron's 8B run processed 150 M tokens in 10 node-hours (4 nodes × 2.5 h), i.e. ~15 M tokens

per node-hour. Two epochs over our 8.3 M tokens ≈ 1.1 node-hours of training; with loading,

checkpointing and the dev generation, budget 2 node-hours per run → the eight runs ≈ 16.

Evaluation is where a suite can silently eat the allocation: the full posttrain suite is 50

tasks including judged arenas and AIME and is not to be run. The per-model eval list below is

sized at ≈ 1–2 node-hours with the vLLM backend. **Cap for the whole first round: 50 node-hours

(2.5% of the remaining allocation)**, single-node jobs only, no 70B, no sweeps beyond the eight

runs, checkpoints kept only for the winner. Every job carries --time limits at that budget.

6. Evaluation per run

  1. Format and stop behaviour on 50 dev prompts: does every answer end with the end-of-turn token, stay in Greek, keep the turn structure? (Gate before any other eval.)
  2. Dev loss per config, and voice: generate answers to the dev prompts and compute the stylometry against no_robots-el (Burrows's Delta, the Biber-style rates, opener census) with the tools already in natural-greek-sft; a pairwise judge (Sol or Claude) of model answer vs the dev reference answer.
  3. Greek: ifeval_greek (541) and ellinika-bench (el, plus en for the English side) are the evals this SFT is for; GreekMMLU is scored with the same log-likelihood scorer as the CPT run, as a retention guard only — it tells us whether the SFT erased knowledge the CPT bought, not whether the model answers exams.
  4. General retention guard with evals-post-train: ifeval, arc_challenge_chat, gsm8k_cot, global_mmlu_gen_0shot (has Greek), include_base_44_gen_0shot (has Greek) — the smoke test Meditron uses for forgetting. We do not train for multiple choice: the data has no exam rows and none are added; a drop on these tasks after SFT is a warning about forgetting (or about the chat format), never a target to optimize.
